## 6. That `ABC-010` closes this defect's trigger — CHECKED, and it holds. Recorded because I opened
the doubt and closing it is owed.

The ticket has said since 2026-07-29 that `ABC-010` *"removes this defect's only reachable trigger by
rejecting the FEN at the boundary"*, and I left that sentence untouched. I had reason to doubt it before
looking: `ABC-022`'s `## Dependencies` records that `ABC-010`'s stated checks do **not** close its half

only the rook's *identity* is wrong.

**Read at the source, and there is no gap.** `ABC-010`'s `## Scope` names, in its own words, *"castling
rights inconsistent with the board — a right asserted with **no king on its home square, or no matching
rook on its home square**"*. That rejects **all three families**: family 1 and family 2 fall to the
king clause (an empty home square and an occupied-by-something-else home square are both "no king on
its home square"), and family 3 falls to the *matching* rook clause, where "matching" carries the piece
type and the colour. So the ticket's sentence stands, and it stands on **firmer** ground than the
`ABC-022`/`ABC-010` pairing does. **No flag is raised against `ABC-010` and it was not edited.**

Downgraded from `UNKNOWN - verify` to "read at the source" and **still recorded in a `claim` note
rather than promoted**, because it rests on `ABC-010`'s *stated scope* — an unbuilt ticket's prose — and
not on running code. What `ABC-010` actually implements is `ABC-010`'s gate to establish.
