# The unexamined soft spot, examined: FIVE defects survive all of `A1`–`A10`

BA/PM named two places it would look first. One — the `option.empty` / `option.kingPath` interaction
— the engineer partly measured. The other, *"a criterion set written entirely over signals that
agree"*, **nobody had examined**, and it was named as the class that has now recurred on `ABC-015`,
`ABC-018` and `ABC-009`. It is examined here, and it is where every real defect I found lives.

## What "signals that agree" means concretely on this ticket

`A3`–`A6` are twelve rows carrying, between them, up to three assertions each: the `FLAG_CASTLE`
count in both layers, the exact `legalMoves` count, and purity over 128 bytes and five scalars.
That reads as thirty-odd independent checks. **It is not.** On every row all of those assertions are
driven by **one binary event** — did this castle option survive the guards. If it survives, the
count is wrong *and* the castle count is wrong *and* (on families 1 and 2) purity fails. The twelve
rows carry twelve bits of discrimination, not thirty-six, and the four rows within a family fall to
the same mutation: measured, deleting the king guard fails `A4`+`A5` as sixteen assertions that are
one fact repeated eight times.

That is not a flaw in itself — repetition across colours and piece types is what makes the table a
table. **The flaw is what a set of agreeing signals cannot show you: the dimensions it holds
constant.** Two are held constant across all twelve rows and both controls, and a defect hides in
each.

## Dimension 1 — the impostor's PIECE TYPE. `A5`/`A6` vary two, and two is not enough.

`## Test notes` 3 asks for *"at least two distinct piece types"*; `A5` uses a bishop and a knight and
`A6` the same. Literally satisfied. But the rule `## Scope` states is *"the moving side's `KING` byte
and no other"* — a statement about **every** other byte. A guard that admits one further own-colour
type therefore satisfies all twelve criteria:

- reading the king's home square as **"the mover's KING **or QUEEN**"** → **337/337 green**,
  typecheck 0, deep lane **32/32**;
- reading the rook's home square as **"the mover's ROOK **or QUEEN**"** → the same.

Both are real defects on positions `parseFen` accepts: **74** and **70** diverging in-scope positions
respectively. The first is the worse of the two, and it is the ticket's own headline harm on the most
valuable piece on the board:

```
4k3/8/8/8/8/8/8/K3Q2R w K - 0 1     delivered  31 moves, 0 castles, position PURE
                                    mutant     32 moves, 1 castle e1g1, and ONE legalMoves call
                                               DESTROYS the white QUEEN on e1 and writes a white
                                               king over it
```

That is family 2 — *"silent material loss and a piece-type change"* — reached with a queen, while
every criterion is green. The queen-on-the-rook's-square form leaves the position **byte-identical**
(family 3's shape), so only the `FLAG_CASTLE` count can ever see it, exactly as `A6`'s rationale
says — and `A6` has no queen row to see it with.

## Dimension 2 — HOW MANY of a side's rights are held at once. Never varied, and this is the deeper one.

Every acceptance FEN and every control holds **at most one castling right per side**. The single
exception is the reproduction FEN `4k3/8/8/8/8/8/8/K7 w KQ - 0 1`, where **both** castles are invalid
and the expected answer is zero castles either way.

So no criterion ever asks: *one of a side's two rights is valid and the other is not — is the valid
one still offered?* And because every row's expected answer is "zero castles", **nothing can
distinguish "this option was rejected" from "the option loop was abandoned".**

Turning either new guard's `continue` into a `break` — or a `return` — is a one-token change that
**silently deletes a legal castle**:

```
4k3/8/8/8/8/8/8/R3K3 w KQ - 0 1     rights KQ; h1 EMPTY so `K` is bogus; a1 rook home so `Q` is LEGAL
                                    delivered  16 moves, 1 castle = e1c1   (correct)
                                    mutant     15 moves, 0 castles         a legal move VANISHES
```

Three mutants of this shape (`break` on the rook guard, `break` on both, `return` on the rook guard)
each read **337/337 green, typecheck 0, and deep lane 32/32**, with **16** diverging in-scope
positions each.

**The asymmetry is the whole lesson, and it is invisible to an agreeing-signal criteria set.** The
same mutation on the **king** guard is genuinely behaviour-equivalent, because both of a side's
options share `kingFrom` (`e1` for `K` and for `Q`). On the **rook** guard it is a defect, because
`rookFrom` differs per option (`h1` vs `a1`). One token, two opposite verdicts, and `A1`–`A10`
returns the same green for both.

## Why `A8` and `A9` cannot cover either dimension, structurally

`A8` (the full suite, whose perft lane asserts exact node counts) is the ticket's named
over-rejection oracle, and `A9` is the deep lane. Both are oracles over **legally-reachable**
positions only — and the engineer's own structural argument explains why they are blind here:
`makeMove` clears a castling right whenever the king or the matching rook leaves home or that rook is
captured, so in any position reachable from a legal start **the right being set implies both pieces
are home**, and the two new guards never fire. A mutation to a guard that never fires cannot change a
perft node. **Measured, not argued: all five mutants above survive `PERFT_DEEP=1` at 32/32.** The
same argument that makes the fix safe for legal play makes the entire perft apparatus unable to
certify it.

## One further hole, honestly NOT an `A1`–`A10` gap, recorded because it is next door

Deleting the **pre-existing** castling-right test — `if ((position.castling & option.right) === 0)
continue;`, the cheapest and most load-bearing line in the function — passes the **whole 337-test
default suite**, including all twelve criteria. It offers a castle on
`4k3/8/8/8/8/8/8/R3K2R w - - 0 1`, where no right is held and everything else is in order (70
diverging in-scope positions).

**`A9` does kill it** — deep lane 31/32, measured — because a king that steps off `e1` and back
leaves both pieces home with the right cleared, which is reachable by legal play. So this is **not a
gap in the acceptance net**; it is a hole in the *default* lane, where the only guard on that
condition is a 40-second opt-in suite. That is a real but lesser finding and it is not charged against
`ABC-009`: the right is not one of the two **board** preconditions this ticket owns.

(19 tests), `src/moves.ts` is **byte-identical** to `8f540cf`

**every one of the fourteen test files that exist on `main` is unmodified** — checked by diffing each
individually, so `A8`'s line-level requirement holds for my work exactly as it did for the
engineer's.

**The rows are load-bearing, proved by re-running the whole campaign with them present:**


and **six mutants die to my rows and to nothing else in the suite**: the two queen-impostor mutants

rejected.
