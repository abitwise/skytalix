# Five things this audit could NOT settle

Everything below is explicitly non-load-bearing. None of it should be quoted as a result, and
none of it should be used to size or sequence a ticket without being closed first.

## 1. Whether the automatic-draw deviation is *acceptable* — a product ruling, not a QE verdict

I established that G-1 **is** a genuine deviation from FIDE 9.2/9.3, and I demonstrated that it
costs a won game in a concrete, reproducible line. I cannot decide whether a single-player CLI
should carry it. That is the owner's call.

What I can say, and what a human should weigh: two decisions already say "automatic is fine"
(`ABC-007` `## Out of scope`, `ABC-019` `## Out of scope` — the latter calling it *"Settled …
not open"*), and **both predate the 2026-07-31 standing priority that reopens the question.**
Whether that priority overrides them is not mine to decide. `UNKNOWN - verify` — needs a named
human.

## 2. Absence of hash collisions over *all* reachable positions

The 518,474-node sweep found zero collisions between distinct positions, and the GF(2)
dependency scan is exhaustive **for position pairs differing in one or two piece-square slots**.
Neither generalises:

- The sweep is depth 3-4 from six positions. It is proof of absence over that sample, not over
  the reachable space.
- The dependency scan says nothing about three-or-more-slot differences. With 781 reachable table
  entries in a 64-dimensional GF(2) space, higher-weight dependencies certainly exist in the
  algebra; whether any corresponds to a pair of positions that can both occur in **one game** is
  unproven.

The analytic bound in R-2 (≈ 2.4 × 10⁻¹⁵ per 300-ply game) assumes the key behaves as a uniform
random 64-bit value, which the table-health result supports but does not prove. My conclusion —
negligible, do not ticket — rests on the analytic bound more than on the sweep. `UNKNOWN - verify`
if anyone ever wants a hard guarantee rather than a practical one.

## 3. Whether all-same-colour-bishops is *universally* mate-impossible

R-1 records `KBB` / `KB` combinations with every bishop on one square colour as dead, matching
the standard table and the implementation. My own derivation is a parity argument: the checking
bishop covers only its own square colour, the orthogonally-adjacent escape squares are the other
colour and no same-colour bishop can cover them, and two kings cannot simultaneously cover both
escapes from a corner.

That argument convinced me and it agrees with the standard table, but **I did not verify it by
tablebase or retrograde analysis**, and it is the one row in the table where the code draws a
game rather than playing on — the direction where an error is unsafe. `UNKNOWN - verify`.

## 4. Whether six is the final count of terminal conditions

`EPIC-006`'s six holds against everything in its stated scope today, and I checked draw by
agreement, loss on time, illegal-move forfeit and stdin EOF against it (see G-5). But
`EPIC-006`'s result screen has never been decomposed into tickets, and its negotiable slice
(save/resume, PGN export) is undecided. A draw-offer command would make it seven.
`UNKNOWN - verify` until `EPIC-006` is decomposed.

## 5. `gameStatus`'s cost at a search leaf when a counts map IS supplied

`gameStatus` recomputes `zobristKey(position)` inside itself on the repetition branch — the audit
measured that call at 288-306 ns. `ABC-007`'s `NFR-001` note promises a leaf-cheap check, and the
single shared legal-move generation delivers that; the key recompute is the one cost that is not
free, and it only fires when a map is passed.

Per `ADR-0002` clause 3 the search will call `gameStatus` bare and decide repetition from its own
per-ply array, so this branch should never fire at a leaf. **That is a reading of an unbuilt
search, not a measurement.** I ran no benchmark. `UNKNOWN - verify` — it belongs to `EPIC-004`,
which does not exist.
