# The `N5` ruling: **option 1 — the bar is RE-DERIVED by its author, and the delivered tree does NOT meet it either.** The breach is accepted as an owned risk, no coverage is cut, and a change that WOULD pass was built, priced and refused

The numbers are in the `observation` note beside this one and are not repeated. This note is the
ruling, the three answers I was asked for, and what I changed.

```
option taken     1 (RE-DERIVE), and because the answer is NOT MET it carries option 2's
                 disposition — an owned risk with a named owner and trigger. Option 3 is
                 refused on a measurement, not on a preference.
N5 re-derived    <= 5% of the default lane's CPU (user+sys), NET of the per-file-count fixed
                 floor, median of paired per-round deltas, n >= 7 interleaved rounds, runner
                 invoked directly, denominator = the lane without this ticket's files, scope =
                 every file the branch adds under src/; admissible only with a null control by
                 identity <= 1% AND a fixed-floor control subtracted and reported.
delivered tree   NOT MET.  gross +13.6% CPU  -  floor +5.8%  =  NET +7.8%  vs <= 5%   (1.6x)
N5 as written    NOT MET on every instrument that resolves; UNRESOLVABLE on the wall clock
                 it names. Both readings agree, which is the point.
```

## Why re-deriving is legitimate here and is not a gate moving a target

Security/NFR stopped in exactly the right place: it delivered the number and refused to re-specify
the bar it had just failed. That rule is not bent here — **it is honoured by the bar's author doing
the re-specification, which is the only role that can.** `plans/nfr-catalog.md` is untouched, no
catalog target moved, and `N5`/`N6` are ticket-level bars that are not rows in it.

There is a real hazard in re-deriving a bar after seeing the measurement, and it deserves a test
rather than a disclaimer. **The test is whether the re-derivation changes the verdict. It does not.**
The ticket fails the original wording on every instrument that can resolve it, and fails the
re-derived wording at 1.6x. The re-derivation makes the bar *measurable*; it costs the ticket, it does
not rescue it. Had it come out the other way I would have had to leave the bar alone and say so.

The precedent is this board's own and it is the same role: **`ABC-013`'s `N1`–`N4` were re-derived by
BA/PM and their SHAPE was changed**, because a *correct* build failed them as written. Here a *null*
change trips the bar. Same defect class — a bar that discriminates independently of the work — same
role, same remedy. The one asymmetry is honest and stated: `ABC-013`'s re-derivation happened before
the work, mine happens after.

## What was wrong with `N5`, including the part no gate named

Security/NFR named five unnamed degrees of freedom and they are all now named. **Two further defects
are mine and were not on anyone's list:**

- **The instrument.** `N5` named the wall clock, which on this lane cannot resolve a delta of this
  size and — measured at this hop — **ranked two empty test files (+6.0%) above this ticket's entire
  24 tests (+4.2%) in the same interleaved run.** That is worse than a noise floor; it is an ordering
  violation between two real conditions. The bar now names CPU (user + sys), whose null control reads
  ±0.1% with the sign at exact chance in all three runs.
- **The threshold had no derivation at all.** `5%` was never derived from what the lane can afford. It
  was the cutting hop's own prediction (0.4–1.2%) plus headroom — **a bar reverse-engineered from an
  estimate** — and the estimate was itself a single-call-form measurement taken in plain `node`
  against a vitest denominator, which the Engineer diagnosed correctly. Nobody had named this because
  everyone was arguing about how to *read* the bar, not where it came from.

And the finding that decides the shape: **two trivial one-assertion test files cost +5.4% / +6.1% of
the lane's CPU.** The lane is dominated by per-file overhead, not test content. **So `≤ 5%` stated
gross is unmeetable by any ticket that adds two test files, including one that adds two empty ones.**
Four instruments measured the delta; none measured the floor of the delta's own species. That is why
the re-derived bar is charged **net** of the floor — it charges a ticket for what it wrote, not for
what the runner costs — and why the floor control is mandatory rather than advisory.

**The `5%` is retained and its status is stated rather than dressed up.** It is not derived from a
lane budget, because this board has no lane budget. It is kept because net-of-floor it is
**measurable** (~50x the CPU null floor) and **achievable** — demonstrated, not assumed: the narrowed
variant measures ~3.8% net. A bar nothing can meet is not a bar; a bar a no-op trips is not a bar;
this one is neither. **Whether 5% is the *right* fraction is `UNKNOWN - verify` until `ABC-026` sets
the total**, and I would rather record that than invent a derivation to look finished.

## Option 3, and why it is refused rather than unavailable

The brief noted that Security/NFR's narrowed `A1` breaches too (+5.3% wall / +7.4% CPU), so if a
change were wanted it had to be a different one. **I found one, built it, and it passes.** `A1` over
this ticket's 23 positions instead of all 207 — two lines, measured green at 676/676, **~3.8% net,
which meets the re-derived bar** — and all five discriminating mutants still die on the 23 alone
while the 23 fill all 44 census cells by themselves.

**I refuse it.** Four reasons, in order of weight:

1. **It is the exact trade this ticket exists to prevent.** `ABC-025` was cut because a thin corpus
   let six wrong builds through 652 of 652. Cutting the corpus by 91% — 7,314 renders to 677 — to
   satisfy a bar its own author wrote is the same failure, in the same file, one ticket later.
2. **Its evidence is the species this board already ruled void, and consistency is not optional when
   the argument favours me.** *"All five mutants still die without the 184 derived positions"* has the
   same defect as the Engineer's *"the counts are identical across all 13 mutants"* — which QE refuted
   and Security/NFR upheld — because the builds that would witness the loss are the ones nobody has
   written. The 184 derived positions are dense real-play boards; the 23 are three-piece ladders. This
   board's standing prior is that the mutant set is never complete: `ABC-023` found fourteen where two
   were predicted, and QE found four suffix-class wrong builds *this* ticket missed. **I will not
   accept for my own bar the argument I upheld QE in rejecting for someone else's.**
3. **The saving is invisible on the axis the harm lives on.** The harm `N5` exists to prevent is the
   engineer's wait. On the wall clock the delivered tree and the narrowed variant are
   indistinguishable. The trade buys a saving no engineer can perceive at the price of the ticket's
   purpose.
4. **The larger lever is not this ticket's.** Two empty files cost 5.4–6.1%. Recovering per-file
   overhead is worth more than gutting `A1` ever could, and it is `ABC-026`.

**If anyone wants that trade anyway it is a human's call and not a role's**, and it is recorded in the
ticket with its price so a human can take it in one reading. `A1` stands as written and as delivered.
Security/NFR's assisted-only variant is not adopted either, for reason 1. **No coverage was cut at
this hop and no acceptance criterion was weakened.**

## The three answers I was asked for

**Risk #7 — the security trigger's unnamed denominator. CONFIRMED, and FIXED here, but NOT with the
one-word fix the gate proposed.** Security/NFR's suggestion was *"say the diff under `src/`"*. That is
wrong in the unsafe direction: **it would exempt `package.json`, `package-lock.json`, `tsconfig.json`
and everything else outside `src/`, so a ticket that added a dependency would not fire the security
half at all** — the one change on this board most worth reading, and the exact class `ABC-013`'s QE
hop was careful to keep out of the repository. The trigger now fires on **the diff outside `plans/`
and `.grugops/`** — the documentation, board and context paths, which have no execution surface.
**Verdict-preservation was checked mechanically before the wording was touched:** +823/−0 over two
files, both `*.test.ts` under `src/`, 0 files failing clause 2 — both clauses still not fired, exactly
as the gate reported. *A trigger may be re-worded only when the re-wording changes no verdict already
rendered.* That was the test and it passed. The literal reading has meanwhile grown from +3,560/−2
across 26 files to **+4,340/−2 across 34**, purely because this task wrote more notes — the defect
demonstrating itself while being fixed.

**Risk #9 — no cumulative budget on the default lane. CONFIRMED, and it is the one with the longest
reach, so it is CUT rather than named: `ABC-026`, in `Backlog`, DoR NOT met.** A named risk owned by
BA/PM with a trigger of *"the next hardening ticket"* fails exactly when the next BA/PM hop does not
read this note; a `Backlog` row does not. Owner **BA/PM with Architect/Design**, because the total
belongs in `plans/nfr-catalog.md`, which Architect/Design seeds — so it cannot reach `Ready` on me
alone, and that is written into the ticket as a DoR gap rather than smoothed. **Its DoR is explicitly
NOT met and the reason is not paperwork: the central number does not exist, and inventing "the lane
may cost N seconds" at a cutting hop is precisely the defect that produced this ticket.** `ABC-024` is
the precedent for parking a leftover with its gap recorded. The floor measurement is the strongest
evidence for why a per-ticket bar can never do this job: **the lane's cost is per-file, and no
per-ticket content bar can see it.**

**Risk #6 re-assignment — CONFIRMED, not disputed, and given a carrier.** Security/NFR's reasoning is
right: a warning whose only owner is a gate that runs *after* the work arrives too late to stop the
engineer deleting the row. It has to be in the ticket the engineer reads. **But "BA/PM at the
`EPIC-003` cut" is a promise about a future hop with nothing to make it happen**, and `EPIC-003`'s
ticket does not exist yet. So it is written onto **`EPIC-003`'s `Backlog` row**, which is what the hop
that decomposes it actually reads — with both things that ticket must inherit: (1) `san.nfr.test.ts`'s
five `N1` call-count rows will go red on a sanctioned optimisation of `toSan`, that is `N1` working as
specified, and the next engineer's cheapest move is to delete the row; (2) it inherits the
**re-derived** `N5` wording and **not** the original, so the next gate does not re-run this argument.
That second clause is Security/NFR's own fourth bullet, given the same carrier.
