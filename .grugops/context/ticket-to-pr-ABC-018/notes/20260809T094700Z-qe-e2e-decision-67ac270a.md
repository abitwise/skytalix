# VERDICT: **PASS**. `ABC-018` `In Review → In Security/NFR` — and why it is not sent back

`In Review` **1/3 → 0/3**. `In Security/NFR` **0/2 → 1/2**. No limit approached, no override taken,
no written reason owed for the count. This is the `In Review` exit, the one board move this role
owns. **Nothing merged and nothing deployed — a human holds both.**

## The verdict, and the standard it was reached against

**All twelve testable acceptance criteria are implemented, covered, and — with the two exceptions
BA/PM already ruled on — non-vacuously tested. NO PRODUCTION DEFECT WAS FOUND.** `src/frame.ts` is
**byte-identical to the Engineer's commit `d88a1f0`** (`md5 bb827f52fe537817e40020a29dfa0e6d`, the
digest they published) after **75 mutate-and-restore cycles**; `git diff --stat d88a1f0 -- src/frame.ts`
is empty. **Everything this role changed is a test.**

The verdict was not reached by reading the module. It was reached by **breaking it**: 56 mutants, 46
killed by the delivered suite, **six that passed all twelve criteria and all 297 delivered tests**,
four that survive and are each disposed of on the record. The two fakes the ticket's own `## Test
notes` name were checked **by construction** and neither is present — a byte-only rewrite of the
screen-model criteria survives the mutant the delivered forms kill, and the criterion that would pass
"even if the writer bypasses the sanitizer" does not, because it asserts on the captured stream.

## Why the six holes are a PASS and not a return to `In Development`

**Not one of them is a defect in `src/frame.ts`.** Each is a mutant the SUITE could not see — a build
that would have been wrong and would have gone green. The delivered build is right on all six. The
fix for a hole the suite cannot see is a test, and the tests are written; **repairing the
implementation to make a verdict green is not this role's, and there was nothing to repair.**

`RISK-005`'s build obligation is discharged and was re-checked mechanically rather than inherited:
the glyph mode is a required parameter with no default, `src/frame.ts` imports exactly one thing
(`import type { GlyphMode }`, erased at runtime), `process.` appears only in prose, and every
`isTTY` / `rows` read is of the **injected sink**. The `mode === "ascii"` comparison inside the
sanitizer is a test of the supplied value, not a source of one.

## The ONE production-shaped risk, named rather than buried — and why it is still a PASS

**`W` is recomputed inside `assembleFrame` on every frame; the ticket calls it *"a session
constant."*** `beginFrameSession` takes `H` and not `W`, so nothing in the module holds a width
across frames. Demonstrated on the screen model, not argued: write a frame whose status panel is 24
wide, then one whose panels are 18 wide, in place — and **the wider frame's tail is still on screen**,
because `ED(0)` cannot reach what sits to the right of a row the new frame overwrote with a shorter
one. **It is the exact class the padding decision exists to prevent, one level up.**

**It is a PASS anyway, and the reasoning is on the record rather than assumed:**

- it **breaks no acceptance criterion** — every criterion is written against one frame's `W`;
- it **cannot fire today**. The only real panel is the merged `renderBoard`, which is always 9 rows
  of 18; the other two are unbuilt and enter as fixed-height stubs. There is no runtime importer of
  `src/frame.ts` at all;
- the fix is an **API change** (a session-level `W`, or `W` as a parameter of `assembleFrame`) whose
  right shape depends on `ABC-016` and `ABC-017`, which do not exist. Deciding it here would be QE
  designing two unwritten tickets;
- and the ticket's own reading is defensible: `W` **is** a session constant whenever the regions'
  widths are, which is true of everything built.

It is pinned by a **characterization test** that documents today's behaviour and fails loudly the day
anyone changes it, and it is routed below. This follows the `ABC-014` precedent, where QE
characterized a deliberate decision rather than silently ratifying or unilaterally changing it.
