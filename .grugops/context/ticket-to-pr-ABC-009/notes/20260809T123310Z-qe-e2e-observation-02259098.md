# The fifth mutant: the colour-blind equivalence argument HOLDS, and no criterion is owed

The engineer handed forward a colour-blind `pieceType` mutant that survives all twelve criteria at
337/337, argued behaviour-equivalence over 9,604 boards with zero divergence, and proposed **no
criterion** for it. My job was to decide whether that argument actually holds. **It holds.** It is
not merely unfalsified — it is corroborated on an instrument built differently from the engineer's,
and it is the correct call to propose no criterion.

## First, the scope boundary the equivalence argument has to be judged against

`## Scope` binds the contract to *"every position it accepts"*. So the question is not "do the two
rules ever read the two squares differently" — they demonstrably do — but "do they ever produce
different output on a position `parseFen` **accepts**".

**Read at the source rather than assumed:** `src/fen.ts` `assertBothKingsPresent` accepts a board
only with **exactly one white king and exactly one black king**, and `parseCastling` performs **no
consistency check at all** between the rights and the board (that is `ABC-010`, unbuilt). So the
in-scope space is: one king each, any castling rights.

**This distinction is load-bearing and my first probe got it wrong.** My initial enumeration built
boards by poking bytes into `emptyPosition()`, which produced boards with zero or two white kings —
`4k3/8/8/8/8/8/8/4Q2R w K - 0 1` has no white king and `parseFen` **throws** on it. A divergence
found there is out of scope and must not be reported as a gap. Every number below is therefore from
a corpus that is round-tripped `toFen` → `parseFen` and **discarded unless parseFen accepts it**:
**12,584 distinct accepted positions, 16,912 candidates rejected as out of scope.**

## The measurement

The delivered tree and each mutant tree are loaded into **one process** and compared on **both
exported layers** plus purity, over all 12,584 in-scope positions. The corpus varies, jointly: every
one of the 13 byte values on `e1`, on `h1` and on `a1`; the white king at `e1`, `a1` and `d4`; **all
four rights combinations including BOTH rights held at once**; and the whole black-to-move mirror
(`e8`/`h8`/`a8`, black king at `e8`/`a8`/`d5`).

```
mutant                                    gen diverge   legal diverge   purity diverge
colour-blind on the KING's square only          0             0               0
colour-blind on the ROOK's square only          0             0               0
colour-blind on BOTH (the engineer's fifth)     0             0               0
```

**Zero divergence, on all three forms.** This is not a re-run of the engineer's enumeration. Theirs
varied the two home squares of one castle plus one arbitrary third piece (9,604 boards); mine varies
three squares jointly, three king placements, both rights at once, and both colours to move, and it
filters to the scope boundary. Two differently-constructed exhaustive probes agreeing on zero is why
I am willing to call this settled rather than merely unfalsified.

## Why it holds — the mechanism, re-derived rather than inherited

The colour dimension of both home squares is **unreachable**, and the reason is stronger than "the
through-check test happens to catch it":

- **The rook's home square.** `option.empty` requires the squares between king and rook to be EMPTY
  and is tested *before* the through-check. So on any board that reaches the through-check test there
  is an **unobstructed line** from the rook's home square along the rank to the `kingPath` squares.
  An enemy rook or queen standing there therefore attacks them **necessarily**, not incidentally, and
  **no blocker can exist** — a blocker would have had to fail `option.empty` first. The engineer
  argued this; it is correct, and it is the reason no third-piece enumeration was ever needed for
  this half.
- **The king's home square.** A king's attack on an adjacent square is unblockable by construction,
  and `f1` (kingside) / `d1` (queenside) are both adjacent to `e1` and both members of
  `option.empty`. So an enemy king on the king's home square always attacks a `kingPath` square.

**Measured beside it, on the delivered tree, so the masking claim is checked and not asserted:** an
**enemy queen** on either home square is likewise already refused —
`4k3/8/8/8/8/8/8/K3q2R w K - 0 1` and `4k3/8/8/8/8/8/8/4K2q w K - 0 1` both offer **zero** castles.

## Therefore no criterion is owed, and writing one would be worse than leaving it

A criterion for the colour dimension of either home square would be **unfalsifiable**: it passes on
`main`'s unfixed tree, it passes on the delivered fix, and it passes on the colour-blind mutant. It
would assert nothing and would pin an implementation shape rather than an observable behaviour. That
is exactly the theatre BA/PM's correction 5 rejected for the wrong-colour rook and the same call
`ABC-011`'s gate made for its two behaviour-equivalent cost mutants. **The delivered fix is stricter
than reachability requires, which is the safe direction, and the stricter form is the honest
statement of the rule.** The engineer's disposition is right on both halves: keep the strict guard,
write no criterion.

I did add two enemy-queen assertions to my hardening file, and they are labelled in the file as **a
recorded measurement rather than a criterion**, precisely because they pass on the unfixed tree too.

## The other eight equivalent survivors, so "15 survived" is not read as "15 holes"

Of the 15 survivors, **nine are behaviour-equivalent in scope** — zero divergence on both layers and
on purity across all 12,584 accepted positions:

| survivor | why it is equivalent |
|---|---|
| colour-blind, king square / rook square / both | unreachable colour dimension, above |
| KING guard `continue` → `break` | **both of a side's options share `kingFrom`** (e1 for `K` and `Q`), so if the king test fails for one it fails for the other — abandoning the loop and skipping the option are the same thing |
| KING guard `continue` → `return` | same reason |
| both guards moved BELOW the empty test | the five tests are ANDed by `continue`; order changes cost, not output |
| both guards moved BELOW the through-check test | same |
| both guards moved ABOVE the right test | same |
| requiring the rook's square non-EMPTY as an extra condition | subsumed — the identity guard already implies it |

Two consequences worth stating. First, the three **placement** equivalents are invisible to the test
suite *and* to `NFR-001`, whose instrument floor the engineer measured at ~±1% per cell — larger than
the cost of moving a byte compare below an attack scan. The engineer's ordering rationale (cheap
tests first) is sound and is **unverifiable by any gate on this ticket**; it rests on reading, which
is the honest place for it to rest.

Second, and this is the crack the next note walks through: the KING guard's `break` is equivalent
**only** because both options share `kingFrom`. The ROOK guard's `rookFrom` differs per option
(`h1` vs `a1`), so the identical mutation there is **not** equivalent — and nothing in `A1`–`A10`
can tell the two apart.

## What this note does NOT establish

- **That the equivalence extends beyond the enumerated corpus.** 12,584 accepted positions with at
  most four pieces is not the position space. The mechanism argument above says no blocker can change
  the result and I believe it, but **only the corpus is measured** — a board with several extra
  pieces was not enumerated by me either.
- **Anything about the placement equivalents' real cost.** No benchmark of mine. `NFR-001` is
  Security/NFR's and I did not re-measure it, as instructed.
