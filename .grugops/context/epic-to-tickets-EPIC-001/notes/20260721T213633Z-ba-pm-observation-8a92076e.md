Three things surfaced during decomposition that were not visible when the epic was written.

**1. EPIC-001 was under-sized, and the estimate is now corrected.** It carried an indicative `L` (5 points) from idea-to-epics. The decomposition totals 22 points across seven tickets. The epic frontmatter and its board row are corrected to `XL` with the correction noted in-file rather than silently overwritten. Consequence for planning: EPIC-001 is the largest epic in the project, not a mid-sized one, and it sits on the critical path for every other epic. Anyone sequencing this project should assume the rules core dominates the early schedule.

**2. The perft expected-value table is a fabrication hazard, and ABC-006 is written to defend against it.** Perft counts are exact published integers; a wrong expected value does not produce a soft failure, it sends an engineer hunting a bug that does not exist — strictly worse than having no test. ABC-006 quotes the widely-published startpos and Kiwipete counts so its acceptance is measurable, but carries an explicit instruction to transcribe the full table from the Chess Programming Wiki at implementation time and treat the wiki, not the ticket, as authoritative.

**3. ABC-005 carries a deliberate do-not-optimize instruction.** Legality filtering by make/test/unmake is the simple correct approach; a pin-aware generator is the fast one. Because RISK-002 (Node search throughput) is unresolved until the EPIC-004 spike runs, the temptation is to pre-optimize here on a guess. The ticket states plainly: correctness first, and the spike — not intuition — decides whether the optimization is ever written. If it is needed, it becomes its own ticket with the spike's measurement as justification.

**Deliberately not done: no System Analyst was activated.** Workflow 03 routes to the System Analyst when behavior is unclear. Chess rules are externally and completely specified by the FIDE Laws of Chess; there is no business behavior to invent or decide, only rules to implement correctly. Activating the analyst here would have produced a restatement of a public rulebook. The `In Analysis` column stays empty for this epic. If a later epic has genuinely ambiguous behavior — engine difficulty curve shaping in EPIC-004 is the likely candidate — that is where the analyst earns its slot.
