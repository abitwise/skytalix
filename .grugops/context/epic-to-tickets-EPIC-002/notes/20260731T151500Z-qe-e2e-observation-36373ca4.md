# `src/gameend.ts` vs the FIDE Laws — five conformance gaps, ranked

Audit of the one surface `npm run perft` cannot reach. Perft counts nodes; it never calls
`gameStatus`, so every terminal and draw rule in this module rests on unit tests and reasoning.
I audited the module against the FIDE Laws of Chess (Articles 5.1, 5.2, 9.2, 9.3, 9.6) and ran
every claim below. **I fixed nothing.** Every reproduction is a FEN or a move list that can be
pasted into a ticket.

Severity is ranked against the owner's 2026-07-31 standing priority — *moves must be legal and
the game played by its rules*. Reachability is stated separately from severity on purpose: one
gap is severe AND latent, and conflating the two is how a board gets mis-sequenced.

---

## G-1 — Threefold and fifty-move are applied automatically; FIDE makes both a **claim**

**Severity: HIGH (a won game becomes a draw). Reachable the ply `ABC-019`'s loop ships — not
today, because `gameStatus` has no production caller.**

FIDE 9.2 and 9.3 make threefold repetition and the fifty-move rule a draw *upon a correct claim
by the player having the move*. Only 9.6 draws automatically, and only at **fivefold** and **75
moves**. `src/gameend.ts:102-108` applies both at three and at fifty, with no claim.

The deviation is not "a draw arrives early". It is sharper than that, and it runs one way:

> **FIDE gives the claim to the player having the move. This code takes the draw away from
> exactly that player.**

Reproduced end to end. Repeated position `P` = `6k1/5ppp/8/b7/8/8/5PPP/4R1K1 w - - 0 1`
(White Kg1 Re1 f2 g2 h2; Black Kg8 f7 g7 h7 Ba5). White to move has **`Re8#`** — verified,
`gameStatus` after `Re8` returns `{"result":"white-wins","reason":"checkmate"}`. Play the
shuffle `1.Kh1 Bb6 2.Kg1 Ba5 3.Kh1 Bb6 4.Kg1 Ba5`:

```
  b6a5  occurrence 3  {"result":"draw","reason":"threefold-repetition"}
  final position : 6k1/5ppp/8/b7/8/8/5PPP/4R1K1 w - - 8 5
  side to move   : WHITE
  Re8 still legal for the side to move: true
  and Re8 is       : {"result":"white-wins","reason":"checkmate"}
```

The third occurrence is created by **Black's** move. Under FIDE, Black has just moved and so
cannot claim (9.2.1 — the claimant must have the move); White has the move and plays `Re8#`.
**FIDE: 1-0. This code: ½-½.**

The fifty-move rule has the identical shape:
`6k1/5ppp/8/b7/8/8/5PPP/4R1K1 w - - 100 60` → `{"result":"draw","reason":"fifty-move"}`, with
`Re8#` on the board for the side to move.

**What it changes for a player.** It hands the side that is losing a drawing resource the Laws
do not give it, and it takes a win from the side that has the move. In a human-vs-engine game
the engine is the side that repeats when it is losing, so the deviation runs systematically
against the human.

**Not covered by the existing suite, and it looks like it is.** `src/gameend.test.ts:142-147`
("prefers checkmate over the fifty-move rule when both could apply") uses a position where mate
is **already delivered**. It does not cover a mate **available to the side to move**, which is
the only case where the deviation bites. A reader of that test will believe this is handled.

**Two sequencing warnings, because this gap is load-bearing for two others.**

1. **FIDE 9.6 becomes required the moment a claim is introduced.** `ABC-007` puts fivefold and
   75-move out of scope on the ground that they are "not needed for a human-vs-engine game where
   the fifty-move claim is applied automatically." **That reasoning is sound today** — verified: a
   knight cycle from the start position terminates at occurrence 3 with the clock at 20, so
   occurrence 5 is unreachable and 150 plies is unreachable. It becomes **wrong** the moment
   claims land, and 9.6 is the automatic backstop that stops a claim-based game running forever.
2. **The automatic fifty-move rule is what rescues G-3.** See below. Make fifty-move claimable
   without also implementing 9.6.2 or 5.2.2 and a locked position never ends at all.

**Disposition — this is a human ruling, not a defect.** The automatic behaviour has been decided
twice already: `ABC-007` `## Out of scope`, and `ABC-019` `## Out of scope` ("*All five terminal
conditions are automatic in this product … Settled by merged code plus `EPIC-006`'s acceptance,
not open*"). **Both decisions predate the owner's 2026-07-31 priority statement.** I am not
reversing them and QE cannot: "is this acceptable for a single-player CLI" is a product call. I
record that it *is* a genuine deviation, that it costs a won game, and that the two rulings that
closed it were made before the priority that reopens the question. **Recommend a human re-read,
not a defect ticket.**

## G-2 — `positionCounts` is optional; omit it and repetition is silently never reported

**Severity: HIGH by consequence, MEDIUM by residual risk. Latent — no production caller exists.**

Confirmed three ways on one position (the start position after two knight round-trips, the
third occurrence):

```
  with a correct counts map : {"result":"draw","reason":"threefold-repetition"}
  counts argument omitted   : {"result":"in-progress","reason":null}
  an empty Map passed       : {"result":"in-progress","reason":null}
```

Same position, three answers, no throw, no return-value signal. The wrong answer is the quiet
one and it is the default.

**Blast radius today, measured rather than asserted:** `gameStatus` has **zero** production
callers anywhere in `src/`. There are 12 call sites, all in `src/gameend.test.ts`, of which 2
pass a counts map.

**On `ADR-0002` clause 4 under a legality-first priority — the disposition stands, the stated
reason does not.** The ADR's Consequences section assigns the trap rather than fixing it, on the
ground that *"making the parameter required would be a breaking change to merged, `Done`,
100%-covered code."* Measured, that break is **9 test call sites and 0 production call sites.**
That is not a reason.

The assignment is nonetheless right, for a stronger reason the ADR does not make: **clause 3 has
the search keep its own per-ply key array and decide repetition itself**, so a search leaf
calling `gameStatus(position)` bare is a legitimate second caller shape, not a mistake. A
required parameter would force the hot path to construct something it deliberately does not
want. **That is the argument that should be on the record**, because a later reader who refutes
the stated reason — as I just did in one grep — will reverse a decision that is correct.

**Residual risk.** `ABC-019`'s acceptance already spy-asserts that *every* `gameStatus` call in a
run carries a counts map. That covers the loop, which is the caller that matters most. It covers
nothing else: `EPIC-004`'s leaf, `EPIC-005`'s book probe and `EPIC-006`'s result screen are all
future callers with no such guard. The mechanism protecting the product's only repetition verdict
is one ticket's spy test plus a convention in an ADR.

## G-3 — FIDE 5.2.2 "dead position" is implemented as a material-count subset

**Severity: LOW. Latent, safe-direction, and self-healing — but only because of G-1.**

FIDE 5.2.2 draws the game the moment *no series of legal moves* can produce checkmate. The code
implements only the material-count subset. Dead positions that are not material-thin are missed.

Reproduced with a fully locked pawn wall — every pawn frozen by the pawn in front of it, no
capture ever available to either side, kings alone on the board:

```
  8/8/8/p1p1p1p1/P1P1P1P1/8/8/K6k w - - 0 1
  gameStatus  : {"result":"in-progress","reason":null}
  legal moves : 3   (a1a2 a1b1 a1b2 — king moves only, forever)
  drives to   : terminal after 117 plies: {"result":"draw","reason":"fifty-move"} clock=100
```

FIDE ends this the moment it arises. The code plays 117 plies of pointless king shuffling first,
then draws for the wrong reason. The error direction is safe — a draw is late, never wrong — and
the delay is bounded at ~100 plies **by the automatic fifty-move rule**, which is G-1's
deviation doing useful work. Remove the automation without adding 9.6.2 and this bound is gone.

Full 5.2.2 detection is not implemented by any engine and is not worth building here. **My
recommendation is documentation, not code.** There is no FIDE article called "insufficient
material"; `insufficient-material` is a subset of 5.2.2, and `src/gameend.ts:1-9` and
`:48-54` describe it as though it were the whole rule.

## G-4 — A stalemate in an already-dead position is reported as `stalemate`

**Severity: COSMETIC. Latent until `EPIC-006`'s result screen names a reason.**

`7k/5K2/5N2/8/8/8/8/8 b - - 0 1` (K+N vs K, Black stalemated) →
`{"result":"draw","reason":"stalemate"}`. Under FIDE the game was already over as a dead position
(5.2.2) before the stalemate arose, so 5.2.2 is the operative article and "stalemate" is the
wrong label. **The result is identical** — a draw either way — and only `EPIC-006`'s result
screen consumes the reason.

**Recorded so that nobody "fixes" it by re-ordering the checks.** The zero-legal-moves branch
running first is what makes checkmate beat every draw rule, and that ordering IS load-bearing
(see R-4 in the companion note). No ticket warranted.

## G-5 — `EndReason` has five values, `EPIC-006` promises six; the sixth is `resignation`

**Severity: NONE as a defect. Confirming `ABC-019`, not re-flagging it.**

The sixth condition is **resignation** — `plans/epics/EPIC-006-game-session-and-packaging.md:24-25`
lists *checkmate / stalemate / repetition / fifty-move / insufficient material / resignation*.

**It does not belong in the rules core.** `gameStatus(position)` is a pure function of a position;
resignation is an event, not a property of a position, and the only producer of an `EndReason`
could never return it. Adding it would make the type describe values its sole producer cannot
produce. `ABC-019` already states the loop must not type its terminal state as `EndReason`
verbatim — I checked that independently and reached the same answer.

**Nothing else is missing.** I checked the other ways a game of chess can end:

| FIDE end condition | In this product? |
|---|---|
| Draw by agreement (9.1) | No — `EPIC-006`'s command set is `undo` / `resign` / `new` / `quit`; there is no draw offer |
| Loss on time (6.9 / 6.10) | No — there are no clocks in any epic |
| Illegal-move forfeit (7.5.5) | Cannot arise — the program only ever offers legal moves |
| Stdin EOF / `quit` | Not a game end — `ABC-019` requires *no terminal result is reported* |

Six is the right number, and five of the six belong here.

## G-6 — inherited, not new: `gameStatus` carries `ABC-009`

`gameStatus` calls `legalMoves` on its first line, so it inherits `ABC-009` in full. Verified:
`4k3/8/8/8/8/8/8/K7 w KQ - 0 1` becomes `4k3/8/8/8/8/8/8/K3K3 w KQ - 0 1` — a phantom second
white king — after **one** `gameStatus` call. Also verified that well-formed positions are
untouched: kiwipete round-trips byte-identically through `toFen` after `gameStatus`.

Already ticketed (`ABC-009`) and already carried by `ABC-019` as inherited exposure. Named here
for one reason only: `gameStatus` is the entry point the loop calls **every ply**, so it is the
highest-frequency route to `ABC-009` in the built product.
