# THE 13 IS MEASURED, NOT ASSERTED — and my first instrument returned 11. Exactly 13 byte values per home square, identical on e1, h1 and a1

The whole termination argument rests on one number: how many values a home square can hold. The
Orchestrator's routing note derives it **arithmetically** — `EMPTY` plus 6 types × 2 colours = 13. That
is a correct count of the *encoding*, but it is not a measurement of the *reachable* domain, and this
ticket has been burned four times by a plausible derivation nobody ran. So I ran it.

## The encoding, read at the source

`src/position.ts` encodes a square as **piece type in the low three bits, `BLACK = 8` as a colour
flag**: `EMPTY = 0`, `PAWN = 1` … `KING = 6`, `BLACK = 8`. `src/fen.ts:90` is the **only** board write
in `parsePlacement` (`position.board[squareAt(file, rank)] = isBlack ? type | BLACK : type`) and
`new Uint8Array(128)` zero-initialises the rest, so the produced set should be
`{0} ∪ {1..6} ∪ {9..14}`.

## The measurement, and the instrument failure that came first

Probe: every code point `0x00`–`0x2FF` as a placement character on the square under test, `parseFen`
run on each, distinct resulting bytes collected with a witness character for each.

**The first version returned 11, and it was wrong.** Its single probe board was
`4k3/8/8/8/8/8/8/K3{ch}2R w K - 0 1`, which already holds a white king on a1 and a black king on e8 —
so `K` on e1 raised *"position must have exactly one white king, found 2"* and `k` raised the black
equivalent. **Bytes 6 and 14 were reported absent from the domain when what was actually absent was a
legal probe board for them.** A limit of the instrument, presented as a fact about the world. Had I
written the criterion on that reading it would have quantified over 11 values and silently dropped the
two KING bytes — one of which is the *required* value on the king's square and one of which is a member
of the equivalence set.

**Corrected with three king configurations per square** (king parked elsewhere; no black king, for
`k`; no white king, for `K`), 62 accepted `parseFen` calls:

```
square          distinct bytes reachable
e1 (index   4)  13   ->  0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14
h1 (index   7)  13   ->  same 13
a1 (index   0)  13   ->  same 13
union over the three home squares: 13.  every square reaches the same 13: TRUE
witnesses: 0 <empty>/"1", 1 "P", 2 "N", 3 "B", 4 "R", 5 "Q", 6 "K",
           9 "p", 10 "n", 11 "b", 12 "r", 13 "q", 14 "k"
```

**So the arithmetic derivation is confirmed by measurement, on all three home squares, with a named
witness per value.** That is what makes "12 wrong values per square is the entire space" a fact about
this codebase rather than a claim about chess.

## The part the arithmetic does NOT capture, and it is not a rounding error

`Position.board` is a **`Uint8Array`**, not a 4-bit field. A square can physically hold **256** values.
The probe measured that **243 of them are never written by any placement character** — the sub-16 ones
being **7, 8 and 15** (`type 7` is unused; `8` is `BLACK | EMPTY`; `15` is `BLACK | 7`).

This matters for honesty about what "exhausted" means. **The 13 is the domain reachable through this
ticket's own `Given` clause**, which is always *"Given the FEN …"*. It is not the raw capacity of the
byte. A criterion over byte 7 or 8 could not be written as a Given/When/Then at all — there is no FEN
that produces one — so it is unfalsifiable for the same structural reason as the equivalence set, and
it is recorded in `A13` under that reason rather than omitted.

## What this observation does NOT establish

- **That 13 is the count for a non-home square, or under a different parser.** Measured on `e1`, `h1`
  and `a1` only, through `parseFen` only. Anything that wrote `position.board` directly is outside it.
- **Anything about behaviour.** This note counts the domain; it does not say what the code does on any
  member of it.
- **Any `NFR-001`, perft or throughput figure.** None measured by this role at any of its four hops.
