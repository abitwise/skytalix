# Sizing the follow-up ticket turned up a FIFTH class: the castle's DESTINATION square is pinned by nothing — and the doubling gap is symmetric, not one-directional

The follow-up ticket had to be sized on this board's own tests rather than on prose, so each of its
three items got mutants. Two items came back as expected. **The third turned up something outside
`A13`'s dimension, and under the stopping rule it is NAMED AND ROUTED to `ABC-023`, not fixed here.**
Same instrument as the companion note: scratch copy outside the repository, unique anchor, bytes
changed, `tsc` per mutant, **one fresh OS process per tree state**, `src/moves.ts` re-hashed to
`0e247853ab2c05887c76105ff83d8621` after every one.

**Why nothing catches it is the whole point.** `legalMoves` filters any move that leaves the mover's
king in check, and a castle landing on an attacked square is exactly that — so the outer layer removes
it and **every count-based oracle, perft included, is blind**. The origin and the crossed square are
*not* masked that way, which is why those four die. What the mutant breaks is the **exported
`generateMoves`** and its own documented invariant (`src/moves.ts:306-308`: *"a castle never appears
when the king would start, cross, or land in check"*), and `ABC-009`'s `## Scope` bullet 4 already
rules that *"offered"* means offered by **both** layers.

**It is the only class found across `ABC-009`'s six hops whose position is LEGALLY REACHABLE** —
everything else on this ticket needs a semantically-invalid FEN. **The delivered code is correct on
it**; this is a hole in the net, not a defect, so no criterion is owed by `ABC-009`.

**Honest limit:** measured on the two **white** options. The two black options are the same
construction in `BLACK_CASTLES` and were **not** mutated — `UNKNOWN - verify` — which is why
`ABC-023`'s `A2` requires rows for both colours rather than inheriting the white result.

**QE's residual is reproduced on my own instrument, including its inheritance claim on `A11`'s own
rows — and the class is wider than "side": a type whose two configurations vary only COLOUR is blind
to a SIDE-conditional admission, and a type whose two vary only SIDE is blind to a COLOUR-conditional
one.** One added configuration per gapped pair — **nine rows** — closes both axes. Neither `A11` nor
`A13` is in shortfall: both require ≥ 2 configurations per type and both have that.

**A recorded near-miss, because the discipline is what makes the numbers usable.** My first run of the
colour-axis probe asserted the condition in the wrong direction (`only when WHITE`, which the white
rows *do* exercise) and returned three kills. The instrument was fine and the prediction was wrong;
inverting the condition to the uncovered direction produced the two survivors above. A red — or a
kill — that contradicts a prediction is a reason to check the probe, not to publish either number.
