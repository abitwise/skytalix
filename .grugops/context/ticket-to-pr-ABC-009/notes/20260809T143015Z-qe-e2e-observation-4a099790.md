# `A11` closed as a DIMENSION: 14 new rows, the quantifier itself asserted, and a seventh derivation of the counts

## First, against my own previous hop, because that is the finding

My `G-1` rows closed the **queen** and I reported the piece-type dimension closed. **It was not.** Four
rows for one piece type is a third sample, not a quantifier. BA/PM then measured three further builds
of the same shape passing all 33 acceptance rows, all 19 of my hardening rows, all 356 tests and the
deep lane — and one of them destroys a **rook** on `4k3/8/8/8/8/8/8/K3R2R w K - 0 1`. The rows that
were not enough were mine. Stating it that way round is the only version of this note worth writing.

## What was added — test rows and ZERO production lines

`src/moves.castling.hardening.test.ts`, the file this role already owns: **19 tests → 34.**

- The two `QUEEN_ON_*` tables became `IMPOSTOR_ON_*` tables that enumerate **every own piece type the
  square can hold**: the king's home square with the matching rook present carries `QUEEN`, `ROOK`,
  `BISHOP`, `KNIGHT`, `PAWN` (12 rows); the rook's home square with the king already home carries
  `QUEEN`, `BISHOP`, `KNIGHT`, `PAWN` (10 rows). **The eight queen rows are carried through verbatim,
  FENs and counts unchanged; 14 rows are new.**
- **Each type appears in at least TWO (colour to move, castling side) configurations** and all four
  configurations appear in each table. Not symmetry for its own sake — measured load-bearing, see the
  companion campaign note's two conditional mutants.
- **The bishop and knight rows deliberately repeat `A5`/`A6` rows.** `A11`'s enumeration must be
  exhaustive **where it is written**; a table that leans on another file for two of its five types is
  not a quantifier, and `A5`/`A6` are not this criterion.
- **One new test asserts THE QUANTIFIER ITSELF.** The piece-type universe is **derived by probing
  `parseFen`** — every letter of the alphabet on two probe boards, because a white piece needs a white
  king elsewhere while the white-`KING` probe needs h1 to *be* that king — and the two tables are
  required to cover that universe minus the legal occupant. Delete a row and it goes red; **add a
  seventh parseable piece type and it goes red until `A11` gains a row for it.** `## Scope` binds the
  contract to *"every position it accepts"*, so `parseFen` is the domain's authority; a second
  hand-written list of types is exactly the sample-shaped artifact this criterion exists to forbid.
- **Every row asserts its own EXPERIMENT before the outcome** (`expectImpostorSetup`): the named own
  piece really stands on the square under test, **the other precondition really HOLDS** so exactly one
  condition can be the reason the castle is refused, and exactly the one right is held.

**Zero production lines.** `src/moves.ts` still hashes `0e247853ab2c05887c76105ff83d8621` and
`git diff 8f540cf -- src/moves.ts` is empty. **All fourteen `*.test.ts` files that exist on `main` are
unmodified**, checked by iterating `git ls-tree -r --name-only main -- src` and diffing each
**individually**; the Engineer's `src/moves.castling.test.ts` is byte-identical. `A8`'s line-level
requirement therefore holds for a third consecutive hop.

## Two shapes stay excluded, carried rather than re-measured — and that limit is named

The mover's own `KING` on the **rook's** home square is not constructible, and an **enemy-colour**
occupant of either square is masked by the pre-existing through-check test. Both exclusions are BA/PM's
and my previous hop's measurements; **I carried them and did not re-derive them this hop**, except that
the two enemy-queen assertions already in the file still pass. The exhaustiveness test encodes the
first exclusion explicitly (`universe` minus `KING` and `ROOK` on the rook's square) so a future reader
meets the reason rather than a silent gap.
