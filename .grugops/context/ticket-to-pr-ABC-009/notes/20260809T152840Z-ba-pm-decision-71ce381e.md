# `A13` ADDED, `A11` NOT WIDENED. The criterion is now a quantifier over all twelve wrong byte values per home square, and the exhaustiveness argument is written into the ticket so it can be checked rather than inherited

The one job: stop adding sample classes and state the quantifier over the whole space. Done, as a new
criterion `A13`. **I agree the space is exhausted for the dimension the criterion quantifies over, and
there is no thirteenth region inside it.** Two regions sit *outside* it and are named, measured and
excluded with reasons — one of them is a bucket the Orchestrator's five-way accounting had no slot for.

## 1. `A13`, not a widened `A11` — and the reason is the audit record, not taste

**Decided: a new `A13`.** Rejected: widening `A11` in place.

- **Widening `A11` would flip a criterion the board certified one hop ago from MET back to NOT MET.**
  `A11`'s `⚠️` marker came off on 2026-08-09 after an independent verification hop that existed
  *specifically* because QE declined to certify its own rows. Re-opening it the same day would destroy
  that record and make the retirement note under `A11` self-contradictory — marker off and back on in
  one file, one day, with the reader unable to tell which verdict is live. My subtask also states
  `A11`'s own-type rows are met and are not to be reopened; widening the criterion they satisfy is
  reopening them in substance whatever it is called.
- **The subject genuinely differs.** `A11`'s subject is *piece type over the mover's own pieces*.
  `A13`'s subject is *the occupant byte* — type × colour, plus `EMPTY`. Overwriting `A11`'s subject
  would erase what was verified and when.
- **A separate criterion carries its own verification state cleanly.** `A13` holds the one open
  `⚠️ NOT YET SATISFIED` marker; `A11` stays retired. QE's next hop discharges exactly one marker.
- **`A13` SUBSUMES rather than duplicates, and says so.** Because it quantifies over the whole space it
  formally covers the nine values `A11` already discharges. Left implicit, a reader would think twelve
  values need rows. So `A13` carries a **value-by-value discharge map**, which is what makes it cost
  two values' worth of rows instead of twelve.

## 2. The exhaustiveness argument, as stated in the ticket

A home square holds **one byte**. `parseFen` can put exactly **13** distinct values in it — measured,
not asserted, on all three home squares with a witness character per value (companion note; my first
probe returned 11 and was wrong). **One** of the 13 is the required piece. So **12 wrong values per
square is the entire space**, and each of the twelve falls in **exactly one** bucket:

```
KING's home square (required byte: own KING)          ROOK's home square (required byte: own ROOK)
EMPTY            -> absence row A4          (1)       EMPTY            -> absence row A3          (1)
own P,N,B,R,Q    -> A11 own-type quantifier (5)       own P,N,B,Q      -> A11 own-type quantifier (4)
enemy BISHOP     -> existing row A5 row 3   (1)       own KING         -> NOT CONSTRUCTIBLE       (1)
enemy KNIGHT,PAWN-> NEWLY REQUIRED BY A13   (2)       enemy BISHOP     -> existing row A6 row 4   (1)
enemy K,Q,R      -> equivalent, EXCLUDED    (3)       enemy KNIGHT,PAWN-> NEWLY REQUIRED BY A13   (2)
                                       total 12       enemy K,Q,R      -> equivalent, EXCLUDED    (3)
                                                                                          total 12
```

**That sentence is what ends the sequence.** Four hops each found one more sample class and each
predicted a fifth; the prediction was right about samples and wrong about the sequence, because nobody
had counted the space a sample was drawn from. Counted, it is finite, small, and now fully measured —
so there **cannot** be a fifth region inside this dimension, as opposed to a hope that there is not one.

## 3. I am correcting the Orchestrator's accounting: SIX buckets, not five, and the two squares partition DIFFERENTLY

The routing note lists five buckets and one table covering both squares. Measured, that is not quite
right, and the discrepancy is exactly the kind this ticket keeps shipping:

- **A sixth bucket exists: `NOT CONSTRUCTIBLE`.** The mover's own `KING` on the **rook's** home square
  while the king is also home needs two kings of one colour and `parseFen` refuses it on all four
  constructions. It is neither behaviour-equivalent (no behaviour to be equivalent to) nor covered by
  any row. Folding it into the equivalence bucket would have been a silent omission of the same shape
  as the one that produced four misses, so it gets its own named slot with its own reason. `A11`
  already carried this exclusion for its own domain; `A13` restates it because the byte accounting
  needs a slot for every value.
- **The two squares do not share a partition.** The routing note's `own QUEEN/ROOK/BISHOP/KNIGHT/PAWN`
  line reads as if it applied to both squares. On the rook's square **own `ROOK` is the required
  value**, not a wrong one, and **own `KING` is a wrong value** that the king's square does not have.
  Both still sum to 12, by different routes: `1+5+1+2+3` and `1+4+1+1+2+3`.

**The conclusion is unchanged and the correction strengthens it** — every value still lands in exactly
one bucket, which is the property the argument needs.

## 4. Do I agree the space is exhausted? YES for this dimension — and NO, I do not claim the criteria set is complete

**There is no thirteenth region inside the dimension `A13` quantifies over:** the occupant byte of one
home square, under that square's own precondition, reachable through this ticket's `Given` clause. 13
values, measured on three squares, all accounted.

Three regions sit **outside** that dimension. I name them rather than let "exhausted" be over-read,
because a blanket claim nobody re-measured is this ticket's signature defect and I will not add a fifth
instance of it:

1. **The 243 byte values `parseFen` never writes.** `Position.board` is a **`Uint8Array`** — a square
   physically holds 256 values, not 16. Bytes `7`, `8`, `15` and everything `≥ 16` are unreachable: no
   FEN produces them, so **no `Given` clause in this ticket's language can reach them** and a criterion
   over them would be unfalsifiable for the same structural reason as the equivalence set. Named,
   measured, excluded with the reason. **This is the region a naive reading of "one byte = 256 values"
   would call a thirteenth region; it is not one, because the acceptance language cannot express it.**
2. **The joint space** — which *pairs* of values co-occur across the two squares, 169 per option, not
   enumerated. For the **delivered code** the joint adds nothing: `addCastlingMoves` tests the two
   squares with two **independent** byte compares (`src/moves.ts:326-327`), no interaction, so
   per-square correctness implies joint correctness by construction. For **mutation coverage** a mutant
   conditional on a pair could survive a finite net — that is a property of any finite net, not a
   region of the byte space, and hop 3 measured two conditional mutants (on colour, on side) that
   `A11`'s rows killed.
3. **The constants no criterion on this ticket varies** — every criterion FEN puts the non-moving king
   on `e8`/`a8` and carries an empty en-passant square with `halfmoveClock` 0 (QE named these two and
   nobody has pursued them), and the `empty`-set squares are a different dimension guarded by the
   pre-existing emptiness test.

**Under the stopping rule, (2) and (3) belong to a follow-up hardening ticket for `src/moves.ts`, not
to another `ABC-009` hop.** I support that rule and this note is a second place it is written down:
`## Scope` is three bullets about castling preconditions, verified across five independent derivations,
and an unbounded mutation net is not in it.

## 5. What QE owes, and what it does not

**Eight rows, zero production lines.** Enemy `KNIGHT` and enemy `PAWN`, on both home squares, at
`A11`'s established doubling (every type in ≥2 configurations, both sides to move, all four rights).
The eight configurations are measured with their setups asserted and their non-inertness checked in the
companion note and in `A13`. **The delivered code already answers every one correctly** — `src/moves.ts`
is still md5 `0e247853ab2c05887c76105ff83d8621`, byte-identical since the Engineer's first commit across
**five** hops now. This is a test-net gap, not a defect.

**One measured warning passed to QE rather than discovered by it:** on the king's square enemy
`BISHOP`/`KNIGHT`/`PAWN` **all answer 13 moves**, and on the rook's square enemy `PAWN`, enemy `BISHOP`
and the **empty** square all answer **5**. A row asserting only its count is interchangeable with rows
for two other values, so the occupant-byte setup assertion is load-bearing on exactly these values.
