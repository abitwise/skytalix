# BLOCKER 2 RULED — the frame is a constant height, the history pane is bounded, and the non-TTY path is a first-class mode

Subtask `ba-pm-epic002-tickets`. This rules the second blocking product decision the System Analyst
escalated (`…-system-analyst-claim-a4b7a2d2` §2), which gated `EPIC-002`'s acceptance criterion #4.
**The System Analyst was right to refuse it** — (i)/(ii)/(iii) is a UX choice, not a behavioural
fact.

## What wave 1 established

Acceptance #4 read: *"Given a 40-move game, when rendering after each move, then the board redraws
without leaving stale rows from the previous frame."*

"Stale rows" is a failure mode of **in-place redraw into a fixed region**, so the criterion
presupposes in-place redraw. But the epic's scope also contained an **unbounded** two-column SAN
history that grows one row per move pair, and the two collide inside one frame. Wave 1's
arithmetic: ~16 fixed rows on a 24-row terminal leaves ~8 for history, so an unbounded pane
**overflows the viewport at roughly move 9 — before the opening is over**, and at the criterion's
own move 40 the frame is ~56 rows, more than twice the viewport. **Above that point the criterion
is not hard, it is undefined:** the frame does not fit the region it is being redrawn into.

That is an overflow arriving at roughly **one quarter** of the move number the criterion itself
names. The criterion could not have been met by any implementation.

## The ruling — four parts, and the first one is the load-bearing one

**1. The move-history pane is BOUNDED to the most recent 8 move pairs, blank-padded.** This is
option **(i)**, and it is what makes the frame a constant height, which is what makes "no stale
rows" mean anything at any game length.

**2. The frame height is a session constant** — it does not vary with move number, message content,
or engine-thinking state. Reference layout: 8 board + 1 file-label + 3 status + 8 history +
2 message + 1 prompt = **23 rows**, fitting a 24-row terminal. The invariant is the criterion; the
exact split is the engineer's.

**3. Two output strategies over ONE frame value, chosen once at startup:** in-place (cursor home +
erase-to-end-of-screen) on a TTY with enough rows; **append** (write the frame, no cursor control
at all) when stdout is not a TTY **or** the TTY reports fewer rows than the frame. This is option
**(iii)** — not as a replacement for in-place redraw, but as the mode that is **forced** anyway:
you cannot redraw in place into a pipe, and `EPIC-006`'s end-to-end test drives stdout as a pipe.
Naming it a first-class mode rather than a degraded fallback is what makes settled rule R12 (the
frame observable as a value, without a terminal) hold, and it also makes the "TTY too short" case a
one-line answer instead of an undefined region.

**4. SIGWINCH is OUT OF SCOPE, and it is a real cut, not an oversight.** Wave 1 named it (stop-list
item 5) as scoped by no epic. Under parts 1–3 a resize does not change *what* is drawn — the frame
is a session constant — and part 3's erase-to-end-of-screen repairs the display on the **next
frame, one ply later**. That makes it an **accepted cosmetic defect** rather than a correctness
problem, and it is recorded in the epic's `## Out of scope` with that reasoning rather than
silently omitted.

### Why not (ii) — history beside the board

It is naturally bounded to the board's ~10 rows, which is attractive, but it introduces a
**minimum terminal WIDTH** requirement the record also does not answer, trading a height question
for a width question and gaining nothing. Wave 1 flagged exactly this cost.

### Why not pure (iii) — append/scroll only, no in-place redraw

It makes acceptance #4 trivially true and it is the cheapest thing to build. Rejected as the sole
model because it does not actually remove the history question — reprinting a *growing* pane under
every board means printing ~20 rows of history 80 times in a 40-move game, which is worse noise
than the problem it solves — and because it discards the "single live board" that is the epic's
stated user value. Taken as the **non-TTY** strategy, where it is correct and forced.

## What was AMENDED on the epic

`plans/epics/EPIC-002-terminal-board-and-game-loop.md`, amendment `[A2]`. Criterion #4 becomes
`4a` (identical row count across every frame of an 80-ply **and a 300-ply** game), `4b` (in-place:
cursor home + one erase-to-end-of-screen; zero rows from the previous frame survive), `4c` (non-TTY
or short TTY: append in full, zero cursor-control sequences, nothing truncated), and:

- **`4d` — REDUCED SCOPE, stated in the epic rather than hidden.** The live frame **no longer shows
  the whole move list**. `EPIC-006`'s game-end screen already promises *"the full move list"*, so
  the information stays in the product — it leaves the live frame only.

**Note that `4a` is a STRENGTHENING, not a softening.** The original criterion asked for 40 moves;
the amended one asserts the invariant at 40 moves **and at 150 moves**, and it holds by
construction rather than by luck. What was reduced is the history scope, not the redraw promise.

## Consequence for `NFR-002` that someone must pick up

Bounding the pane changes the frame from **O(plies)** to **O(window)**. Arithmetic over wave 1's
measured per-row figures — replay to the window's first ply at 2.2–3.9 µs (flat, dominated by one
`parseFen` at 1.62 µs) plus 16 visible plies at 4.31–4.45 µs each — puts the pane at **≈ 70 µs at
any game length**, against 171 µs / 533 µs / 1,300 µs at 40 / 120 / 300 plies unbounded. Apple M3,
Node v24.12.0, darwin/arm64; `UNKNOWN - verify` on any other hardware.

**This is BA/PM arithmetic over someone else's measurement. It is a target, not a result.**
`ABC-017` owns proving it, and asserts it as a **ratio** (the 40/120/300-ply figures within 2x of
each other) as well as an absolute, because the ratio is machine-independent and the absolute is not.

**`plans/nfr-catalog.md`'s NFR-002 row is now stale and I did not edit it.** It states that *"the
frame is a function of GAME LENGTH, not only of position — the history panel is O(plies)"*, which
was true of the unbounded model. That row is Architect/Design's — **flagged, not fixed** (AGENTS.md
rules 9 and 12). It is owed an update when the renderer is built, and `ABC-018` is the ticket that
finally measures the half of NFR-002 the row itself calls *"the half nobody has measured"*.

## This ruling also disposes of the ticket boundary the routing note was waiting on

The routing note said the redraw model *"determines whether the loop skeleton and the renderer are
one ticket or two"*. Under this ruling they are **two**: the frame is a **value** (`ABC-014` /
`ABC-016` / `ABC-017` build it, `ABC-018` assembles and writes it) and the loop (`ABC-019`) decides
**when**. `ABC-018` is buildable and testable against stub panels of fixed height, so the height
invariant is provable before any real panel exists.

## Confidence

**High.** The bound is forced by arithmetic wave 1 measured against a viewport size that is not in
dispute, and the two-strategy split is forced by `EPIC-006`'s pipe-driven test, which exists in the
record. The judgment calls are the pane's **8 pairs** and the accepted SIGWINCH defect; both are
recorded with their reasoning and both are cheap to revisit.

**`UNKNOWN - verify`: the write half of `NFR-002`.** Nothing about frames has been measured, no
renderer exists, and no number for terminal write time may be quoted from any note in this task.
