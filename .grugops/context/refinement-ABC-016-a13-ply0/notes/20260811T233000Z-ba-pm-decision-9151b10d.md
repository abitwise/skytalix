## 1. The replacement wording, as written into `A13`

The struck sentence:

> "**and both counting clauses are measured at a ply depth of AT LEAST 2**, the scan clause stated
> ply-independently: **the spied `squareAt` count for one render is `64` plus the number of occupied
> squares in `history.initialFen`**, at **every** depth asserted."

The replacement, now in `plans/tickets/ABC-016-status-panel.md` under `A13`:

> **and the scan clause is: one render's spied `squareAt` count is the sum of exactly two terms,
> NEITHER of which grows with the number of plies —**
> - **the material scan: exactly `64`, on EVERY render** — one `squareAt` per square of the board,
>   once, whatever the board holds and however deep the game;
> - **the last-move replay: exactly the number of occupied squares in `history.initialFen`, paid
>   once on exactly the renders where the `toSan` clause above counts `1`, and `0` on the empty
>   history where that clause counts `0`.** The replay's `makeMove` steps cost nothing — `makeMove`
>   never calls `squareAt`.
>
> **So the count is `64` when `history.moves` is empty, and `64` plus the anchor's occupied squares
> when it is not.**
>
> **Where each clause is asserted**, stated separately from what it counts:
> - **the scan clause: at ply 0 AND at a depth of AT LEAST 2**, each on **two anchors of different
>   occupancy**. Both regimes are required and neither substitutes for the other. Ply 1 is not
>   required.
> - **the `isInCheck` clause: its DIRECT half at ply 0** — as that half's own words already say —
>   **and its DIFFERENCE half at a depth of AT LEAST 2.**
>
> **The "AT LEAST 2" requirement governs the difference half and the scan clause's non-empty regime.
> It does not reach the two halves written on an empty history, and never did.**

### Is a depth-dependent form the honest answer? NO — and that framing is itself part of the defect

The brief offered a depth-dependent form as an acceptable honest answer. **I decline it, because the
count does not depend on depth at all.** Measured at this hop, depths 0-5 on two anchors: the value
moves exactly once, between 0 and 1, and never again. **The split is not "depth 0 vs deeper" — it is
"there is no last move to render" vs "there is one."** Writing it as depth-dependence would encode a
true statement with a false cause, and the next reader would look for a depth term that does not
exist.

The two terms are each **depth-invariant**. The replay term is **regime-conditional**, on exactly the
guard `A13` already names twice: `history.moves.length === 0` (`src/status-panel.ts:154`).

### "Ply-independent" is retired as a phrase

What it was buying is **depth-invariance** — the count does not grow with the game — which is what
makes the ≥ 2 requirement meaningful rather than a second magic number, and what kills a scan running
twice from ply 2 on. **That property is untouched.** What the phrase wrongly implied is
regime-invariance, which was never true and was never needed. The D22 rationale sentence that
asserted it is **struck rather than deleted** in the ticket, with the reason attached.

## 2. The retained test: FOLDS INTO `A13`. It stays where it is; `A13` now claims it

**Disposition: folded — the assertion becomes criterion-required, and the `it` block stays exactly
where and as it is.** It is not promoted to its own criterion and it is not left orphaned.

The decisive reason is **not** economy, it is the NFR bar. `## Security / NFR triggers` part 3 names
*"the call counts of `A13`"* as the part of the bar that actually binds. **Folding keeps that
reference complete with zero NFR edit. A new criterion would put the only assertion that catches the
empty-replay build OUTSIDE the named bar** — which is the `D17` shape this ticket has already been
burned by twice: a bar naming a subset of what binds.

Supporting reasons: it is the same counter, the same render and the same instrument — one property in
two regimes, not two properties; and `A13` already splits on the empty history **twice** (the `toSan`
clause's *"0 on an empty history"*, and the direct `isInCheck` half). The scan clause was **the one
clause of the three that failed to carry the carve-out its siblings carry.** That is the tell, and it
is why the fix is obviously right rather than a matter of taste.

Leaving it as-is-and-unclaimed was rejected outright: an assertion no criterion owns is the mirror of
`D17` — cover the ticket has but does not claim — and the next hop that trims tests has no ticket text
telling it the assertion is load-bearing.

## 3. Engaging the engineer's counter-reading, which the brief asked me not to skip

The engineer offered two readings and asked for one line to settle it. **Reading 1 (the clause scopes
to the depths it asserts) is UPHELD as the intended one.** But I do not adopt its supporting framing,
on two points:

**(a) The engineer called this "under-specification at an edge BA/PM had no reason to visit." That is
too generous and I am not taking it.** Under-specification is silence. This sentence was not silent:
it stated a formula and quantified it with *"at **every** depth asserted."* **It was false at ply 0,
affirmatively.** The distinction matters because it changes the remedy — silence is closed by adding
a line, a false statement is closed by replacing one, which is what I did. And "an edge I had no
reason to visit" is not accurate either: **the empty history is this panel's most-visited edge**;
`A13` carves it out twice, two lines above the sentence that ignored it.

**(b) Reading 2 is weaker than the engineer allowed, and it is worth saying why.** It says the clause
binds *every* `squareAt` assertion in the file, making the retained test a contradiction. Under
reading 2 the criterion forbids an assertion that catches a build nothing else catches — so reading 2
does not merely lose an argument, **it makes `A13` self-defeating**, since `A13`'s entire reason for
being a call count is to catch cost regressions no output can see. A reading that turns a criterion
against its own stated purpose is not a live alternative. The engineer said a reader taking reading 2
"is not obviously wrong"; I would put it more strongly — such a reader is wrong, but **the text gave
them every excuse**, and that is my defect, not theirs.

**Where I agree with the engineer without reservation:** retaining the assertion and flagging without
amending was the correct boundary, and the decision to **measure the cost rather than assert it** is
what made this hop decidable in one pass. Had it simply written "I think this should stay," I would
have had an opinion to weigh instead of a number.
