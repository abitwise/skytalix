## The ruling

`A13`'s `isInCheck` clause is **replaced**, not deleted and not restated as a total. Exact new text
is in `plans/tickets/ABC-016-status-panel.md` under `A13`; the operative words:

> **the panel makes exactly 1 `isInCheck` call of its OWN** — asserted in **both** of the two ways
> below, because neither alone is enough:
> - **directly, on an empty history:** the render's **total** spied `isInCheck` count is **exactly
>   1**. `toSan` is not called there (the clause above pins that), so the panel is the render's only
>   caller and the total **is** its own count. **This clause names no other module.**
> - **by difference, on a history of at least one move:** the render's **total** spied `isInCheck`
>   count, **minus** the count that a bare `toSan(position, lastMove)` makes on the **same move**
>   against the **same position**, measured in the same file, is **exactly 1**.

Every other clause of `A13` is **untouched**: the four zero-counts, `toSan` 1 and 0 on an empty
history, and the 64-square scan once.

## Why not either of the two obvious amendments

The engineer offered three and recommended `(a)`. I take `(a)`'s *intent* and reject all three
*wordings*.

- **`(b)` "1 on an empty history and 2 thereafter" — REJECTED.** It makes this ticket **restate a
  number `src/san.ts` owns**, so a change to SAN's suffix strategy turns `ABC-016`'s suite red for
  another module's reason. That is precisely the fault `ADR-0009` clause 1 names in the other
  direction — *the producer owns its count; a consumer must never restate it* — and it is what this
  board struck `A11`'s `frameHeight(...) is 23` clause for **nine hours ago**. Writing it back in on
  the same ticket, on the same day, would be incoherent.
- **`(c)` drop the clause — REJECTED.** The four zero-counts catch a returning repetition derive, but
  **nothing else on this ticket catches a panel that asks the board about check twice**, and
  `## Security / NFR triggers` names *"the call counts of `A13`"* as the part of the bar that
  actually binds. Deleting an inconvenient criterion is not a ruling.
- **`(a)` "exactly 1 from the panel itself" — INTENT ACCEPTED, WORDING REJECTED.** *"From the panel
  itself"* names **no instrument**, and with a module-level spy the panel's own call is not directly
  observable on a non-empty history. Two readers would build two different tests, which is exactly
  the DoR failure the criterion is being repaired for. This ticket names its instrument everywhere
  else — `A5` names *when* the `toFen` snapshot is taken, `A12` names that it must be asserted on
  returned rows and **never** through `assembleFrame` — so `A13` names its two.

**Judging the engineer's delivered test, as the brief asked.** The subtraction is **sound and more
robust than it first looks**: it holds whatever `suffix()` does internally, because both sides move
together. Its one real weakness is that it is a *derived* number whose meaning lives in the test's
construction rather than in the ticket's words — so the fix is to put that construction **into the
criterion**, which is what the replacement does. Its second weakness, that a build calling `toSan`
twice and `isInCheck` zero times itself would also read a difference of 1, is already closed by the
`toSan` **exactly 1** clause beside it. **It was right to build it and right to say plainly that it
was not the criterion as written.**

## Does this change the code? For `A13`, NO

`src/status-panel.ts` was already correct — the criterion's arithmetic was wrong, not the panel.
**Both halves of the new clause are already asserted by delivered `src/status-panel.nfr.test.ts`**:
the direct half at `:130-132`, the difference half at `:140-149`. So `A13` goes from *"not fully met,
one clause defective"* to **met on delivered code with no line changed**, and the delivered test stops
being a stated departure from the criterion and becomes the criterion.

**`A13` is not why this ticket moves backward.** A second defect is — see this hop's `observation`
note on `D19b`.

## Preserved on purpose

`A13` still fails if anybody puts the repetition derive back into the panel. That property lives
**entirely** in the four zero-counts (`gameStatusOf` / `repetitionCounts` / `keyHistory` /
`gameStatus`), which this amendment does not touch. Worth stating because it is easy to assume the
`isInCheck` clause carried some of it: it never did — `gameStatusOf` reaches `isInCheck` only through
`gameStatus`'s zero-legal-moves branch, so on almost every position a re-added derive would move the
`isInCheck` count by **zero**.
