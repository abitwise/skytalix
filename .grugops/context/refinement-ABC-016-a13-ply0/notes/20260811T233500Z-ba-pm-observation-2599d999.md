## The regime boundary, measured at every depth 0-5 on two anchors

`squareAt` calls in ONE render of the **delivered** panel:

| anchor | ply 0 | ply 1 | ply 2 | ply 3 | ply 4 | ply 5 | `64 + occupied` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 32-piece start | **64** | 96 | 96 | 96 | 96 | 96 | 96 |
| 3-piece sparse | **64** | 67 | 67 | 67 | 67 | 67 | 67 |

**The value moves exactly once, between ply 0 and ply 1, and never again.** The struck formula is
false at ply 0 by the anchor's piece count (32 and 3 respectively) and true everywhere else.

**The ply-0 count is `64` on BOTH anchors** — the empty regime is occupancy-independent as well as
depth-independent. That is a sharper fact than "it is 64", and it is why the ply-0 assertion has real
discriminating power of its own: a scan walking the *occupied* squares instead of all 64 would read
32 and 3 there.

Component costs, measured separately so the two-term decomposition is not an inference:

| component | 32-piece anchor | 3-piece anchor |
|---|---|---|
| `parseFen` alone | **32** | **3** — exactly the occupied count |
| `makeMove` alone | **0** | **0** — at any depth |

Read at source: `src/status-panel.ts:152-159`, `lastMoveSan` returns at `if (moves.length === 0)`
**before** it reaches `parseFen`. That single guard is the whole cause.

`isInCheck` totals in one render — **1** at ply 0, **2** at plies 1-5, both anchors. The
**difference** (render total minus a bare `toSan` on the same move against the position the panel
replays to) is **1** at every depth 1-5 on both anchors.
