# BLOCKER 1 RULED — glyph mode: Unicode by default, two stream-level signals, and `--unicode` as the override

Subtask `ba-pm-epic002-tickets`. This rules the first of the two blocking product decisions the
System Analyst escalated in `…-system-analyst-claim-a4b7a2d2` §1, which gated `EPIC-002`'s
acceptance criterion #2. **The System Analyst was right to refuse it** — the default and the signal
set are product calls, not behavioural facts readable off the record.

## What wave 1 established, and what I am NOT allowed to invent

Acceptance #2 read: *"Given a terminal without Unicode support (or `--ascii`), when the board
renders, then the ASCII fallback is used and no replacement glyphs (`?`/`�`) appear."*

Wave 1 established that **"a terminal without Unicode support" is not observable from inside the
process.** A process can read whether stdout is a TTY, what the locale claims, and the platform. It
**cannot** read whether the installed font has glyphs for `U+2654`–`U+265F`, and it cannot observe
what the terminal drew. So the criterion asserts a property of the rendered pixels, which no
in-process test can assert.

**I have not invented a detection mechanism.** Every signal in the ruling below was named as
observable by wave 1. Nothing here probes a font, and nothing here claims to know what a terminal
displayed.

## The ruling

**Precedence** is settled rule R8 (`…-system-analyst-decision-cca3c342`) and I do not touch it:
explicit flag > capability signal > default, decided once at startup, cached, never re-probed.
R8 deliberately left *the default and the signal set* open. Those are what I am filling in.

**Default: Unicode, on every platform.** The product's value is "you glance at the terminal and
instantly read the position", and the figurines are what delivers it. Every current default
terminal on macOS, Linux and Windows 11 renders them.

**Two signals switch it to ASCII. Both are about the STREAM, not the font** — which is the honest
line between what a process can and cannot know:

| # | Input | Mode |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `--ascii` | ASCII |
| 2 | `--unicode` | Unicode |
| 3 | no flag, `process.stdout.isTTY` falsy | ASCII |
| 4 | no flag, TTY, first **set** of `LC_ALL`/`LC_CTYPE`/`LANG` does not match `/utf-?8/i` | ASCII |
| 5 | no flag, TTY, locale unset **or** UTF-8 | Unicode |

**`--unicode` is added.** The System Analyst named this exactly: under any automatic rule a wrong
detection has no override today, because only the ASCII direction has a flag. Adding one is the
cheapest thing in this entire ruling and it is what makes a wrong auto-detection a one-word fix
rather than an unplayable game. The two flags are mutually exclusive.

### The three options wave 1 laid out, and why each landed where it did

- **(a) default ASCII on Windows, Unicode elsewhere — REJECTED.** `process.platform === "win32"` is
  a proxy for "legacy conhost", and Windows Terminal is the default on Windows 11 and renders the
  figurines correctly. The proxy is therefore **wrong on the majority of Windows machines today**,
  and it would hand the largest desktop platform the worse board to protect a shrinking tail.
  Wave 1 flagged this cost itself. The table above carries a **regression guard** in `ABC-015` on
  this decision, because "add the platform check" is the obvious thing a future reader will try.
- **(b) default Unicode everywhere, `--ascii` the only escape — TAKEN, and then strengthened.**
  Wave 1's honest objection to (b) was that it *"makes acceptance #2's automatic clause vacuous"*.
  Rows 3 and 4 are what stop it being vacuous: a non-TTY sink and a declared non-UTF-8 locale are
  two real, common, in-process-observable conditions under which the figurines are the wrong
  choice, and neither is a font guess.
- **(c) ask once on first run, and remember — REJECTED, and this is the one reversible choice.**
  It is the **only** option that observes what the terminal actually drew, so it is the only one
  that could keep the original promise. Rejected because it puts a prompt in front of a product
  whose value proposition is "one command, five seconds, and you are playing" (and `EPIC-006`
  promises `--colour black --difficulty 3` produces **no prompts** at all), and because remembering
  the answer requires a persistence surface this product does not otherwise have. **A human who
  values the promise above the five-second start reverses exactly this one choice and nothing
  else** — recorded in the epic as criterion `2c`.

### Why row 3 (non-TTY → ASCII) is not just a testability convenience

It is the right product answer on its own terms: a sink that cannot be seen and cannot be asked
should receive the portable form. That it also makes `EPIC-006`'s pipe-driven end-to-end test
deterministic is a genuine second benefit, not the reason.

### Why row 4 tests "set and not UTF-8" rather than "unset"

Unset is the normal Windows case and carries no information. Treating unset as a negative signal
would smuggle option (a)'s platform rule back in through the side door while looking like a locale
rule.

## What was AMENDED on the epic, and the promise that was reduced

`plans/epics/EPIC-002-terminal-board-and-game-loop.md`, amendment `[A1]`. Criterion #2 becomes:

- **`2a`** — in ASCII mode, the count of code points above `U+007F` in the emitted frame is exactly
  **0**. This is settled rule R9's restatement, and it is a **strengthening**: it is byte-exact,
  decidable, and asserted by the snapshot tests the epic already called for. A terminal cannot
  substitute a glyph it was never sent.
- **`2b`** — the five-row selection table above, asserted as nine unit tests with the environment
  injected and no TTY attached.
- **`2c` — REDUCED PROMISE, stated in the epic rather than buried.** The product **no longer
  promises that a replacement glyph never appears.** On a UTF-8 TTY whose font lacks the figurines,
  Unicode mode draws tofu and no signal predicts it. The remedy is `--ascii`, documented in
  `--help`. **Deliberately not a row in the frame** — a hint row would break criterion `4a`'s
  constant frame height, which is the other blocker's whole mechanism.

**Flagged for named-human ratification, and NOT blocking anything.** `[A1]` reduces a user-facing
promise, and this repo's standing convention is that a material promise change is ratified by a
named human — `ADR-0002`, `ADR-0003` and `NFR-005`'s amendment all were. It does not block cutting,
`Ready`, or development, because the amended criterion is testable either way. Same posture
`ADR-0004` carries and for the same reason.

## One adjacent item ruled because it decides whether `2a` is a complete statement

**ANSI colour is cut from the MVP; light/dark shading is by CHARACTER.** Stop-list item 4 asked
whether `--ascii` also disables colour and whether `NO_COLOR` is honoured. It is entangled with
this blocker: ANSI escapes are themselves ASCII, so a coloured frame satisfies `2a` while still
being unusable in a pipe and unassertable in a snapshot. Cutting colour disposes of `NO_COLOR`, of
the TTY/pipe divergence, and of the question entirely, and it keeps the epic's stated scope item
("light/dark square shading") intact by delivering it with a character instead. **Deferred, not
deleted** — `RISK-005` still names colour and the epic's `## Out of scope` records where it went.

## Confidence

**High on the mechanism**, which uses only signals wave 1 established are observable and invents
none. **Medium on the default**, which is a judgment about a terminal population I have not
measured — I have no data on what this product's users run, and I have stated that rather than
implied otherwise. The judgment is cheap to reverse: it is one row of a table, and `--unicode`
means a wrong call is a one-word fix rather than an unplayable game.

**`UNKNOWN - verify`: whether `WT_SESSION` or any similar environment variable reliably identifies
Windows Terminal.** It was considered as a sixth signal and left out — I could not verify it from
this session, and a signal I cannot verify is a guess wearing a table row. Recorded so that whoever
does verify it knows it was considered rather than missed.
