# THE GAP — three builds pass all eleven criteria, all 439 tests AND deep perft 32/32, because the acceptance tables SAMPLE two axes that `## Scope` quantifies

This is the finding the gate exists for, and it is the sixth appearance on this one ticket of the
shape this board keeps paying for: **a criterion narrower than the ticket's own `## Scope`.** It is
the first of the six that is a hole in the delivered TESTS rather than in prose.

The delivered fix reads exactly two board bytes plus the side to move. Each byte is an axis. The
acceptance tables move along the **colour** axis on both and along the **piece-type** axis on
neither.

## Axis 1 — the byte on the en-passant target

`A1` names a knight and a rook on both colours, which satisfies *"at least two distinct piece types"*
literally. `## Scope` bullet 1 says **"whatever occupies it and whatever colour it is"** — a claim
about every byte. So a guard that admits one further own type survives.

**`t-queenOK`** reads the target as *"EMPTY, or the mover's own QUEEN"*. Typecheck exit 0.
**439/439. Deep lane 32/32.** It offers `e5d6` on `4k3/8/3Q4/3pP3/8/8/8/4K3 w - d6 0 1` and the
mirror on `4k3/8/8/8/3pP3/4q3/8/4K3 b - e3 0 1`, and playing it overwrites the mover's own queen with
a pawn — `ABC-009`'s "most valuable piece on the board" harm, one function over.

## Axis 2 — the byte one rank behind the target, and this is the sharper hole

`A3`'s six rows put **EMPTY** or the mover's **OWN pawn** on the capture square and nothing else. Its
own words are *"an en-passant target with no capturable pawn behind it"*, which an enemy knight
satisfies — the rows just never go there. `## Scope` bullet 2 requires **"the enemy pawn"**.

**`t-enemyAny`** reads it as *"some ENEMY piece is behind it"*. Typecheck exit 0. **439/439. Deep
lane 32/32.** Measured, FEN printed and re-parsed:

```
4k3/8/8/3nP3/8/8/8/4K3 w - d6 0 1   gen_ep=1 legal_ep=1
   one legalMoves call:  board[67] 10 -> 9      the enemy KNIGHT is rewritten into a PAWN
   playing the move:     4k3/8/3P4/8/8/8/8/4K3 b - - 0 1     the knight is simply gone
4k3/8/8/8/3pR3/8/8/4K3 b - e3 0 1   gen_ep=1 legal_ep=1
   one legalMoves call:  board[52] 4 -> 1       the enemy ROOK is rewritten into a PAWN
```

Eight configurations across bishop, knight, rook and queen on both colours to move, every one
impure. The mechanism is exactly half (b)'s: the move is emitted with `captured: PAWN | …` and
`undoSpecial` — **untouched by `ABC-022`** — writes that byte back unconditionally. The delivered fix
is what stops it, and nothing in the net notices if a build stops stopping it.

**`t-notOwnPawn`** reads it as *"anything that is not the mover's own pawn is behind it"* — weaker
still. Typecheck exit 0. **439/439. Deep lane 32/32.** Sixteen corrupting configurations, and
**four of them read CLEAN across the whole `legalMoves` call and DIRTY per make/unmake pair** — the
self-masking phenomenon again, on rows nobody has tested. A call-level-only oracle would have missed
those four even with the right FENs.

## What the delivered code actually does, so the gap is not mistaken for a defect

Both axes were quantified against the delivered tree over every FEN-producible piece code:

```
axis 1  the byte on the target        22 configurations   wrong: 0
axis 2  the byte behind the target    22 configurations   wrong: 0
king rows, kings relocated             8 configurations   wrong: 0
purity across the call and per pair   40 positions        impure: 0
```

**The shipped generator is correct on all 44.** `parseFen` refuses a board with two kings of a
colour, so the king rows relocate the kings rather than being skipped; bytes that are not valid piece
codes are not FEN-producible at all and stay out of every in-repo instrument's reach.

## Why perft cannot help and no perft position should be added

Every configuration above needs an en-passant square the board contradicts, and there are 0 such
nodes in 518,474 reachable ones. The deep lane passes all three survivors at 32/32. `## Test notes`
item 4 is right and this measurement is a third confirmation of it.
