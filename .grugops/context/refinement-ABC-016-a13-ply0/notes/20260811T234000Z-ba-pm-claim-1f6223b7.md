# The three judgements a gate cannot adjudicate, and the pattern behind two corrected rulings

> **Hand-authored.** A `claim` rather than an `observation`, and the split is the one QE and the
> engineer both drew on this ticket: **the measurements underneath are not in doubt** — they are in
> this hop's `observation` note and every one was run — but *"this is the right amount of criterion
> to write"* is a product judgement. `confidence: UNKNOWN - verify` is on the **dispositions**, not
> on the readings.

## 1. Folding the ply-0 assertion into `A13` rather than making it a criterion

**The judgement:** it is one property in two regimes — same counter, same render, same instrument —
and the `## Security / NFR triggers` bar names *"the call counts of `A13`"*, so a new criterion would
put the assertion outside the named bar.

**The honest counter:** the two regimes catch **different defect classes**. The ≥ 2 regime catches a
scan that runs twice; the ply-0 regime catches a replay that should not happen at all. Someone who
weights defect-class over instrument would give the second its own criterion and edit the NFR bar to
name both. **That reader is not wrong** — they would buy sharper traceability for one more criterion
and one NFR edit. I took the cheaper side because this ticket's failure mode has repeatedly been the
bar naming a subset of what binds, and an NFR edit is one more place for that to happen.

## 2. NOT returning the ticket to `In Development` for the comment

**The judgement:** the amendment leaves `src/status-panel.nfr.test.ts:241-253` framing a
now-criterion-required assertion as *"RETAINED DELIVERED COVERAGE"*, with a phrase pointing at
retired vocabulary. I checked every sentence and **none becomes false**, so I ruled it a provenance
staleness and recorded it as `D27` for the next dev hop that opens the file anyway.

**The honest counter, and it is a real one:** this ticket has **measured evidence** that stale
comments on this exact file suppress real work — the `A13`-is-unsatisfiable sentence demonstrably
stopped QE from probing the depth. A comment reading "RETAINED" where the criterion now says
"REQUIRED" invites precisely the deletion this hop exists to prevent. **A reader who weights that
delete-risk above the cost of a dev hop would send it back now, and I cannot call them wrong.**

**Why I did not:** the root cause is addressed where it belongs — `A13` now names the ply-0 assertion
as required, and the test's own name states the property — so the comment is the second line of
defence, not the first. Against that, a dev hop plus re-gate on the ticket that **merges first** and
that **three tickets wait on** is disproportionate to a comment whose every sentence is true.
**If the Orchestrator weighs the merge-order cost differently, sending it back is legitimate and I
would not re-argue it.** This is the single place in this hop where a defensible person lands
elsewhere.

## The pattern behind two corrected rulings, offered as a check rather than an excuse

The brief noted I have now twice ruled something a later hop had to correct. Having looked at both,
**they are the same defect, not two:**

- `D19` — the original `A13` `isInCheck` clause stated a blanket total (*"exactly 1"*) across one
  regime where it is 1 and another where it is 2.
- `D22` — the depth sentence stated a blanket depth (*"AT LEAST 2"*) across clauses that include two
  deliberate ply-0 regimes, and a formula that is false in one of them.

**Both are one quantifier written across an unexamined edge regime, and on this panel the edge regime
is always the same one: the empty history.** `A13` carves it out three times now; the two defects
were both in the clause that failed to.

There is a second-order pattern worth naming too: **both defects were in a REPAIR, not in an original
criterion.** A repair arrives with the authority of having just fixed something, and it gets read as
a solution rather than as a new claim. It is a criterion like any other and earns the same
adversarial reading.

**The check I would apply, and did apply at this hop:** for every quantifier in a criterion, name the
input regimes it ranges over and evaluate it at each boundary — then say in the text which regime
each clause is asserted in. Applying it here found the second instance (the direct `isInCheck` half)
that the engineer's flag had not named. **Whether that check generalises beyond this panel is
unproven, and it is offered as a habit rather than a rule.**
