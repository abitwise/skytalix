## T for `simple-tournament` (Q4 with Q8): proposed 22 000 ms; the ceiling of 22 500 ms does not move
- Each move costs `s = T + o`. `o` is the in-process overshoot (≤ ~31 ms, `AR-NFR004-A`) plus the pipe and GUI latency
  (UNKNOWN - verify; `ABC-222` A6 reads it).
- Clock left after a period's 40th move: `c = 900 000 − 40(T + o)`.
  - Q8 needs `c ≥ 500`, so `T + o ≤ 22 487.5`.
  - Q4 needs `T + 31 ≤ 22 500`.
- At T = 22 500: `c ≈ 50 − o` ms, via the `go` rule's `remaining − 50`. That misses Q8.
- At T = 22 000:
  - `c = 20 000 − 40o`, which is 18 760 ms at `o` = 31. It holds while `o` ≤ 487 ms.
  - Headroom under the ceiling: 500 ms a move.
  - M2: 97.8 % of a period, and 83.1 % with 6 book moves (book `maxPly` 12). It fails only with ≥ 10 free moves in a
    period.
  - The cost: 2.2 % of the clock.
- Under 40/15 with `movestogo`, today's `go` share stays ≥ 22 500 ms on every move, so a GUI cap (if the Architect
  rules one) gives T.
- Without `movestogo`:
  - with a cap: about 165 s left and 81.6 % used;
  - with no cap: 18.8 s left and 97.8 % used.
  - So M1 and M2 hold either way.
- 22 450 would leave 760 ms, which is too thin for an unread latency on 5 games.
- **The owner is told:** "22.0 s a move, never more than 22.5 s; about 18–19 s left at each period's end; about 98 %
  of the clock used." The Architect may refine T in the ADR. The page follows his ruling, and the owner is told.
