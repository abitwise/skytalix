# `ABC-009`'s acceptance, corrected in place: seven defects, three named by no pre-check

The Definition of Ready was re-derived at the `Ready → In Development` pull. **The
*acceptance measurable* row is a FAIL, not the amber the pre-check left, and the *NFR triggers* row
was a tick on an unmeasurable clause.** Both are met only after the corrections below. Four of the
seven are the Orchestrator's four (`20260809T103247Z-orchestrator-decision-f050eb4b`); **three were
named by no pre-check**, and one of those three survives the pre-check's own proposed fix. The
`ABC-018` precedent of 2026-08-09 was five where three were named; this is seven where four were.

**No board move.** `ABC-009` has been in `Ready` since 2026-07-31 and stays there; `Ready` stays
**3/8**. The `Ready → In Development` exit is the Orchestrator's, was **not** taken, and no engineer
was enqueued. **Nothing merged, nothing deployed.**

**Nothing else moved.** Size `S` and priority `P1` re-checked and unmoved; the diagnosis, `## Problem`,
`## Source`, the escalation block, the escalation trigger and its unspent status are untouched.
**`ABC-022`, `ABC-010`, `ABC-011`, `ABC-013`, `EPIC-001` and `plans/nfr-catalog.md` were NOT edited.**

The evidence is in the two companion `observation` notes and is not restated here. Each ruling below
names the alternative it rejected, in the `ABC-018` house style.

## The four the pre-check named

**1 — The headline harm had no criterion, and the gap is FOUR rows wide, not two. AGREED, widened.**
A fix implementing the rook precondition alone passes all six criteria, the suite at 304/304 skip 0
and the deep lane at 32/32, while still writing a phantom king. `A4` now carries four rows — both
colours, both sides — with the post-fix count (16) and the three-call signature. **Rejected
alternative:** widen `A3`'s FEN to strip the king as well. That makes one FEN fail both preconditions
at once, which is the subsumption the test notes now forbid — a criterion that can fail for either
reason pins neither.

**2 — Purity asserted through `toFen`. Substance AGREED, implication CORRECTED DOWNWARD.** `toFen`
reads 64 of the 128 bytes, which is right. But the castling route **cannot write off-board**, and a
128-byte diff over all twelve leaking rows and the whole perft table found zero off-board changes —
so **this is a criterion-strength change and NOT a live-defect fix**, and nobody should hunt an
off-board write in the castling branch on the strength of it. It changes anyway for three reasons that
are not "it hides a bug": `ABC-022` asserts this property over 128 bytes and five scalars **in this
same module**, and two siblings asserting one property two ways is a reader trap; `## Scope` says "the
position" and `toFen` is a lossy render of it; and it is free. **Rejected alternative:** keep `toFen`
alone and note the limitation in `## Test notes` — a note is not a criterion, and `ABC-018`'s QE found
six mutants living in exactly that gap.

**3 — `## Scope` bullet 1 overlapped `ABC-022` and nothing fenced it. AGREED.** Measured: with a
correct castling fix in place, `ABC-022`'s half (b) reproduces unchanged, so bullet 1 taken literally
is not met by any castling fix and an engineer honouring it must edit `ABC-022`'s fix site. Bullet 1
is now scoped *"on the castling route"*, the reciprocal out-of-scope line names `ABC-022` with a
concrete fence (no line of `addPawnMoves`, no `FLAG_EN_PASSANT` behaviour), and `## Dependencies` names
`ABC-022` back. **Rejected alternative, and rejecting it is the point:** edit `ABC-022`'s acceptance to
absorb bullet 1. That is the inversion `ABC-018`'s correction rejected in its own item 2 — closing a
gap in ticket A by editing ticket B — and `ABC-022` is a pullable `Ready` ticket whose acceptance is
not this refinement's to move.

**4 — The `NFR-001` bar had an unmeasurable clause. AGREED, and the baseline was WRONG, not merely
inconsistent.** *"Does not regress materially"* is not a number; the bar is now the **ratio to the same
measurement on the pre-fix tree in the same process, ≥ 0.98**, taken from `ABC-022`. Verified at the
source: `plans/nfr-catalog.md` line 26 — the authority — records the 2026-07-29 re-measurement as
**6.04M–6.21M** over five runs and `ABC-022` carries 6.04M; this ticket's **6.09M** matched neither.
Corrected, with the machine and `UNKNOWN - verify` elsewhere, plus the catalog's benchmarking caution
and its 97.3%-free-leaves denominator fence. **No target moved** and **no throughput number is mine** —
`npm run perft` was not run. **Rejected alternative:** re-measure here to settle the baseline. BA/PM
does not own the NFR gate, one run is the wrong instrument by the catalog's own caution, and a ratio
measured in one process makes the question moot — which is the argument for the ratio form.

## The three no pre-check named

**5 — Both preconditions are IDENTITY tests and no criterion could tell identity from OCCUPANCY. This
is the worst of the three.** Measured: testing both home squares for non-emptiness passes all six
original criteria, the suite at 304/304, the deep lane at 32/32, **and all four of correction 1's new
rows**. Eight rows still leak, in two families with opposite visibility. **Family 2** (a non-king on
the king's home square) is *worse* than a phantom king — the occupant is destroyed and overwritten
with a king of the moving side, so one `legalMoves` call is silent material loss plus a piece-type
change; measured on an own bishop, an own knight, an **enemy** bishop and a black bishop. **Family 3**
(a non-rook on the rook's home square) leaves the position **byte-identical**, so **no purity
criterion, however strong, can ever see it** — only a `FLAG_CASTLE` count can. `A5` and `A6` are
therefore both required and neither substitutes for the other. This is `ABC-022`'s own lesson (`A1`
piece-agnostic, *"at least two distinct piece types and both colours to move"*; `A5` the enemy-occupant
control) and `ABC-018`'s (*"named so the fix is not written as 'reject own-colour occupants'"*).
`ABC-009` is older than both and inherits them here rather than being the one place that does not.
**Rejected alternative:** leave it to the engineer's judgement, since *"the king on its home square"*
obviously means the king. It is not obvious to a test — the criteria are the specification, two builds
that read it differently were both green, and this board has now shipped this exact class three times.
**One shape deliberately NOT specified:** a wrong-colour **rook** on the rook's home square is
unreachable (an enemy rook there attacks the squares the castle needs empty, so the existing
through-check test already drops the option — measured). A criterion for it would be theatre.

**6 — Every criterion was written at the `legalMoves` layer while the ticket's own reproduction places
the mechanism in `generateMoves`.** *"No castling move is offered"* had no stated subject, so a filter
at the `legalMoves` layer satisfies it while the exported `generateMoves` keeps emitting a move the
rules forbid — two correct-to-acceptance builds differing observably, the `ABC-018` defect-1 shape.
It matters beyond tidiness: `ABC-022` pins the identical class at **both** layers (`A1`/`A2`), so a
`legalMoves`-layer fix here would leave the two halves of one defect class fixed at two different
layers in the same module. `A3`–`A6` now assert the count for **both**, which costs one clause and is
free to test — both functions report the same castle count on every leaking row today. **Stated because
it cuts against the finding:** `generateMoves` has no non-test caller other than `legalMoves`, so a
`legalMoves`-layer fix would be observationally equivalent *for the product as it stands*. That argues
for pinning the layer, not against it — a requirement should not rest on a caller list `EPIC-004` will
change. **Where the guard goes is still not decided**; `addCastlingMoves` versus anywhere else remains
the engineer's, exactly as the pre-check left it.

**7 — `A2` named no post-fix number, and the only number in view is a pre-fix one a correct fix
changes.** `A2` asserted only that three calls return the same number, with *"(Today: 5, 10, 10.)"*
beside it. Measured on all three candidate fixes: the post-fix count is **3, 3, 3** — the king on a1
has three moves and both of today's castles are phantom — **so 5 is not the answer at any point after
the fix.** `A3`, one line below, *does* state its post-fix count, so an engineer mirroring its shape
writes `expect([5, 5, 5])` and ships a test a **correct** fix fails. `A2` now states **exactly 3** and
`A1` gains its own counts. **Rejected alternative:** leave equality alone because `A1`'s purity
assertion forces the count anyway. It does not — `A1` constrains the position, not the number, and the
number is what the test asserts.

  failed of 304, kiwipete on all four). `A8` already is that criterion; `A7` is not and now says so.
- **No `gameStatus` criterion**, though the escalation block, the traceability row and QE's gap `G-6`
  all lean on that route. Derivable, and derivability was **checked** (`src/gameend.ts:137`;
  `hasLegalEnPassant` pure by construction since `ABC-011`), so a criterion would restate `A1` in a
  second caller.
- **No `NFR-005` trigger** — structural, not measured: `zobristKey`'s per-node cost runs through
  `hasLegalEnPassant`, which calls neither `generateMoves` nor `legalMoves`. Recorded so nobody re-runs
  the key benchmark to "confirm" it.
- **The security classification is unchanged** — re-asked rather than carried. Correction 5 found
  silent material loss, which is a worse *reliability* consequence and not a security one.
- **The 100%-coverage lesson in `## Problem` stands exactly as written.** Correction 5's mutants are
  its second proof: three observably different builds, all green against 304 tests.
