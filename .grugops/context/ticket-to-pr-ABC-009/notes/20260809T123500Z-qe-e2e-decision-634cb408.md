return to dev. The distinction is the whole point of this note, so it is stated first and plainly:

**I found five defects that survive the entire acceptance net, and NOT ONE of them is a defect in the
delivered code.** All five are defects in the *criteria*. The delivered fix is correct on every one
of the positions that expose them — it is the mutants that are wrong. So there is nothing for the
engineer to fix, and sending this back to dev would be sending back a correct implementation.

## Why not a clean pass

`A1`–`A10` is **not** a complete net, and that is now measured rather than suspected. Five mutants
pass all twelve criteria, all 337 delivered tests **and** the `PERFT_DEEP=1` lane at 32/32, and all
five diverge from the delivered fix on positions `parseFen` accepts. Two criterion gaps produce them,
both of them instances of BA/PM's *"criterion set written over signals that agree"*:

- **`G-1` — the impostor's piece type. `A5`/`A6` vary two types; the rule quantifies over all of
  them.** A guard reading either home square as "the mover's KING/ROOK **or QUEEN**" is green on
  everything. On `4k3/8/8/8/8/8/8/K3Q2R w K - 0 1` one `legalMoves` call **destroys a queen** and
  writes a king over it — the ticket's own family-2 harm, on the most valuable piece on the board.
- **`G-2` — the number of rights held per side. Never varied.** Every criterion FEN carries at most
  one right per side except the reproduction FEN, where both castles are invalid. So no criterion asks
  whether a valid castle survives when its sibling is bogus, and nothing separates "this option was
  rejected" from "the option loop was abandoned". `continue` → `break` on the rook guard **deletes a
  legal castle** on `4k3/8/8/8/8/8/8/R3K3 w KQ - 0 1` (16 moves / 1 castle becomes 15 / 0) and is
  green on all twelve criteria. The same mutation on the **king** guard is genuinely equivalent,
  because both options share `kingFrom` — one token, two opposite verdicts, one green.

Both are **closed in tests only**. `src/moves.castling.hardening.test.ts` is new (19 tests, all
passing on the delivered fix); `src/moves.ts` is **byte-identical** to `8f540cf`; the engineer's
`src/moves.castling.test.ts` is untouched; all fourteen test files on `main` are unmodified, each
checked by an individual diff. Re-running the campaign with the rows present: **47 → 53 killed, 15 →
9 survived**, and **six mutants die to my rows and nothing else in the suite**. The nine
behaviour-equivalent survivors still survive — my rows kill zero of them, which is the property that
proves they pin behaviour rather than implementation shape.

## The ruling on the fifth mutant, since it was handed to me to decide

**The colour-blind equivalence argument HOLDS, and no criterion is owed.** Corroborated on an
instrument built differently from the engineer's — 12,584 `parseFen`-accepted positions varying three
squares jointly, three king placements, **both rights at once**, and the black-to-move mirror, versus
their 9,604 over two squares plus one extra piece — with **zero** divergence at `generateMoves`, at
`legalMoves`, and on purity. The mechanism is sound and stronger than "the through-check test happens
to catch it": `option.empty` guarantees an unobstructed line from the rook's home square to the
`kingPath` squares, so an enemy rook or queen there attacks them **necessarily** and no blocker can
exist; and a king's attack on an adjacent `kingPath` square is unblockable by construction. A
criterion for the colour dimension would pass on `main`'s unfixed tree, on the delivered fix and on
the mutant alike — **unfalsifiable, and therefore theatre**. The engineer's disposition was right on
both halves: keep the stricter guard, write no criterion. **Rejected alternative:** add a
wrong-colour row anyway "for symmetry" with `A5`'s enemy-bishop row. Rejected because the enemy
*bishop* row is falsifiable and an enemy *king/rook/queen* row is not — measured, not assumed:
`4k3/8/8/8/8/8/8/K3q2R w K - 0 1` and `4k3/8/8/8/8/8/8/4K2q w K - 0 1` both offer zero castles on the
