# What the audit REFUTED — six suspicions that did not survive contact

A gap list is only credible if the things that turned out fine are reported with the same
force as the things that did not. Six candidate defects were tested and cleared. Two of them
were on the Orchestrator's suspicion list.

---

## R-1 — `isInsufficientMaterial` is exactly correct. 18 combinations, zero mismatches.

The FIDE test for a dead position is **"mate is impossible"**, not "mate cannot be forced". The
two differ, and the difference is where every engine gets this wrong. This implementation gets
it right in every combination I could construct.

Each row was checked against the standard mate-impossible table (`code=draw` means
`insufficient-material` was returned; `fide=dead` means mate is impossible):

```
  OK  K vs K                               code=draw fide=dead
  OK  KN vs K                              code=draw fide=dead
  OK  KB vs K                              code=draw fide=dead
  OK  KNN vs K                             code=play fide=play
  OK  KNNN vs K                            code=play fide=play
  OK  KN vs KN                             code=play fide=play
  OK  KB vs KN                             code=play fide=play
  OK  KBN vs K                             code=play fide=play
  OK  KB(c1) vs KB(f8) BOTH colour 0       code=draw fide=dead
  OK  KB(c1) vs KB(c8) opposite colours    code=play fide=play
  OK  KBB(b1,f1) both colour 1 vs K        code=draw fide=dead
  OK  KBB(c1,f1) opposite colours vs K     code=play fide=play
  OK  KBB(b1,f1 col1) vs KB(c8 col1)       code=draw fide=dead
  OK  KBB(b1,f1 col1) vs KB(f8 col0)       code=play fide=play
  OK  KBN vs KB (all bishops col 1)        code=play fide=play
  OK  KP vs K / KR vs K / KQ vs K          code=play fide=play
  mismatches: 0
```

Three results are worth naming individually because each is a classic defect this code does not
have:

- **KNN vs K plays on.** Two knights cannot *force* mate, but a mate position exists, so FIDE
  does not call it dead. Calling it a draw is the single most common insufficient-material bug in
  hobby engines. This code does not.
- **Same-coloured bishops on OPPOSING sides draw** (`5b2/4k3/…/2B1K3`), and **opposite-coloured
  bishops on the SAME side play on** (`…/2B1KB2`). The implementation keys on square colour
  across the whole board rather than per-army, which is the correct reading — and the two cases
  are easy to swap, which is why both are now pinned by tests.
- **KBB on one colour vs KB on the other plays on.** One bishop of the other colour is enough to
  make mate constructible, and the code sees it.

**No false draw is reachable, by construction.** The two draw branches are `minors <= 1` — which
after the early `PAWN`/`ROOK`/`QUEEN` return can only be K vs K, K+B vs K or K+N vs K, all dead
— and `knights === 0 && bishopColours.size === 1`, which is all bishops on one square colour,
also dead however many there are. Neither branch can over-apply.

**Verdict: refuted. The Orchestrator's request to check "every material combination including
KNN vs K, opposite-coloured bishops, and same-coloured bishops on opposing sides" came back
clean on all three.** Nine of the eighteen rows are now in the suite (see the artifact-ref note).

## R-2 — The false-draw risk from 64-bit hash identity is negligible. Fourteen orders of magnitude below `ABC-011`.

This is the Orchestrator's suspicion 2 — the *other* direction from `ABC-011`'s under-detection.
Tested three ways; all three clear it.

**Table health.** Rebuilt the key table from the same seed and the same draw order.
1,933 entries: **zero zeros, 1,933 distinct.** Of those, 781 are addressable by a legal position
(12 valid piece bytes × 64 on-board squares, plus side-to-move, 4 castling and 8 ep-file keys) —
all 781 distinct, and **zero weight-2 or weight-4 GF(2) dependencies** across all
C(781,2) = 304,590 pairs, checked exhaustively. That is the check that matters for chess:
a weight-4 dependency would mean **two positions differing in exactly two piece-square slots
collide**, which is trivially reachable on a board. There are none.

**Empirically.** Walked the six standard perft positions (startpos d4, kiwipete d3, position-3
d4, position-4/5/6 d3): **518,474 nodes, 296,322 distinct FIDE-identity positions (placement +
side + castling + ep square), zero collisions between distinct positions.**

**Analytically.** A false threefold needs `count(key) >= 3` while the position itself has
occurred fewer than three times. The dominant term is "the position occurred twice and one other
position collides with it": ≈ C(300, 2) · 2⁻⁶⁴ ≈ **2.4 × 10⁻¹⁵ per 300-ply game**, and that is
an upper bound, since not every collision lands on a twice-repeated position. The
three-distinct-positions term is ≈ 10⁻³².

Compare the direction `ABC-011` already tickets: the en-passant over-distinction fires with
probability ≈ 1 after essentially any double push. **The two error directions are more than
fourteen orders of magnitude apart.**

**Verdict: refuted. Do not spend a ticket on it, and do not widen the key.** 64 bits is the right
identity mechanism here. What is wrong with the key is `ABC-011`'s *semantics*, not its width.

## R-3 — The Zobrist key's FIDE-identity components are complete, `ABC-011` aside

FIDE's "same position" test is same side to move, same pieces on same squares, same available
moves — which means castling rights and a *real* en-passant right. The key XORs placement, side
to move, the four castling flags and the ep **file**, and excludes the move counters. All four
components are present and nothing extraneous is.

The file-only ep component is **sufficient**, not a shortcut: the ep square's rank is a function
of the side to move (rank 3 with Black to move, rank 6 with White to move), and side to move is
hashed separately. The one thing wrong here is `ABC-011` — the component is included when the ep
square is merely *set* rather than when a capture is *available* — and that is already ticketed,
already `Ready`, and out of my scope.

## R-4 — Checkmate-before-draw ordering is correct, and it is correct cheaply

`legalMoves(position).length === 0` runs first and splits on `isInCheck`, so checkmate and
stalemate are both decided before any draw rule can fire. A mate is never lost to a high clock,
to thin material, or to a third repetition. Verified at clock 100
(`4Q2k/6pp/8/8/8/8/8/6K1 b - - 100 80` → `checkmate`, `white-wins`) and at clock 100 with a
stalemate (→ `stalemate`, which is also correct: FIDE 5.2.1 ends the game immediately).

It also does this from a **single** legal-move generation, which is what `ABC-007`'s `NFR-001`
note demanded. Nothing recomputes.

**This ordering is load-bearing and must survive any fix to G-4** (the cosmetic reason-name
divergence in the companion note). Re-ordering the checks to make a dead position outrank a
stalemate would risk exactly the bug this ordering prevents.

## R-5 — The halfmove-clock boundary is exact

```
  clock= 98  in-progress      clock=100  fifty-move
  clock= 99  in-progress      clock=101  fifty-move
                              clock=150  fifty-move
```

100 plies is 50 moves by **each** player, which is what FIDE 9.3 requires; 99 is one ply short
(Black has made only 49). `>= FIFTY_MOVE_PLIES` is the right comparison and 100 is the right
constant. Now pinned by three new tests, including 99 — the boundary the existing suite
approached through a played move but never asserted directly.

## R-6 — FIDE 9.6 really is unreachable today, so `ABC-007`'s out-of-scope note is sound

`ABC-007` excludes fivefold and 75-move on the ground that they are not needed while the
fifty-move claim is automatic. I did not take that on trust. Driving the knight cycle five times
from the start position, the **first** terminal verdict lands at occurrence **3**
(`threefold-repetition`) with the clock at 20. A game that stops at three can never reach five,
and a clock that resets long before 150 can never reach 9.6.2.

**The reasoning is sound and the exclusion is correct — while the claim stays automatic.** It
becomes wrong the same day G-1 is addressed. That conditional is the whole content of this
result; recorded so nobody re-derives it, and so nobody carries the exclusion forward past the
condition that supports it.
