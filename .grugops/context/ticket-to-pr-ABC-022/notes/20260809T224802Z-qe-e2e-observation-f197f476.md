## The disjoint split, by set arithmetic on failing test NAMES

Failing names extracted from vitest's JSON reporter, compared with `comm` and `cmp`:

```
delete the empty-target condition   ->  8 named tests
delete the captured-pawn condition  -> 23 named tests
intersection                        ->  0 tests   (EMPTY)
union                               -> 31 tests   BYTE-IDENTICAL to the unfixed control's 31
```

Neither condition subsumes the other and together they explain the unfixed tree's failures exactly,
with nothing left over. `## Test notes` item 3 is discharged.

**All 31 of the unfixed control's failures are inside the new file**, so `A9`'s "no existing test is
modified" is not merely achievable — no existing test pinned the defective behaviour at all.

## `A5` EARNS ITS KEEP — the claim that justifies keeping it is true

The colour-shaped restructure was rebuilt from BA/PM's description rather than copied: an own-colour
`continue` at the top of the capture loop, the ordinary-capture branch's own `continue` dropped in the
same rewrite, and the emptiness clause gone from the en-passant guard so every enemy occupant falls
into it. Measured on `t-restr`:

- `tsc --noEmit` exit 0
- passes `A1`, `A2`, `A3`, `A4`, `A6`
- **435 of the 439 delivered tests pass**, and the only four failures in the entire suite are `A5`'s
  four rows — both knight rows and both rook rows, both colours
- `PERFT_DEEP=1` **32 passed (32)** — the deep lane is blind, as the ticket says, because an occupied
  en-passant target is unreachable in legal play (0 of 518,474)

**`A5` is the only thing on this board that fails this mutant.** Its replacement justification is
therefore true, and the widening to two piece types on both colours is doing work.

Purity survives on the `A5` rows even while the bad move is offered, and the reason is structural
rather than lucky: `makeMove` reads `undo.captured` from the board's `to` square before overwriting
it, so `unmakeMove` puts the occupant back. That is the ticket's own *"pure is true of the pair and
says nothing about the consequence"*, confirmed here — which is exactly why `A4` cannot stand in for
`A5`.

## Two criteria worth naming for what they do and do not kill

- **`A6` is not decoration.** `t-wrongDir` — reading `to + forward` instead of `to - forward` — is
  caught by `A6`'s two control rows inside the file, and by 14 further tests outside it. It is the
  only mutant in the campaign that the over-rejection guard catches first.
- **`A2` killed nothing that `A1` or `A3` did not already kill**, across all fourteen trees. That is
  not a defect: the ticket already states `A2` is structurally implied by `A1`/`A3` because
  `legalMoves` is a strict filter over `generateMoves`, and keeps it because the pass-through is the
  surprising part. Recorded so the redundancy is a measured fact rather than an assumption.
- **`A4` never uniquely killed anything either** — every mutant it caught, `A3` caught too. Also not
  a defect: `A4` pins the *consequence*, and the consequence is the harm this ticket exists to stop.
