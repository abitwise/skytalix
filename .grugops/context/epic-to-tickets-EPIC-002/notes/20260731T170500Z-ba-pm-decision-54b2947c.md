# G-1 is ruled ON: two tickets, one coupling that must not be split, and two reversals stated out loud

QE established that G-1 **is** a genuine FIDE deviation and recorded explicitly that whether it is
*acceptable* is a product ruling it could not make (`...claim-aab64fc7` §1, `UNKNOWN - verify —
needs a named human`). **This note is that ruling.** It is taken under the project owner's standing
priority, Olger Oeselg, 2026-07-31:

> *"The most important thing is that chess moves are legal according to game rules, everything else
> comes after that. Game has to be played according to it's rules."*

**The ruling: the deviation is not acceptable, and it is corrected.** Threefold repetition (FIDE
9.2) and the fifty-move rule (9.3) become **claimable by the player having the move**; fivefold
(9.6.1) and 75 moves (9.6.2) become the **automatic backstops**. `plans/tickets/ABC-020-*` and
`plans/tickets/ABC-021-*` carry it.

## The two tickets

| Ticket | Title | Epic | Size | Priority | Column |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **`ABC-020`** | FIDE 9.2/9.3 draws become claimable, and FIDE 9.6 automatic backstops land with them | `EPIC-001` | **`M`** (3) | **`P0`** | **`Ready`** |
| **`ABC-021`** | Draw-claim interaction — the player having the move claims, the loop ends the game, the engine does not | `EPIC-002` | **`S`** (2) | **`P1`** | `Backlog` (behind `ABC-020`, `ABC-019`) |

`ABC-020` is `EPIC-001`'s because it changes `src/gameend.ts`, the rules core — the same place
`ABC-009` and `ABC-011` sit. `ABC-021` is `EPIC-002`'s because it is one outcome added to
`ABC-019`'s state machine.

**`P0` for `ABC-020` is the directive applied, not deference to it.** This board's standing
latent/live test would give `P1` — `gameStatus` has zero production callers, so nothing in flight is
broken. Two facts make `P0` the honest rank anyway: the deviation costs a won game **on a legal
position reached by legal play** (every other correctness ticket here needs a semantically-invalid
position or has a measured-safe error direction), and it has a close deadline — `ABC-019` and
`ABC-016` encode today's semantics in their acceptance criteria.

**`P1` for `ABC-021`, deliberately not `P0`.** `ABC-020` alone leaves the product fully conformant:
no draw is imposed that the Laws do not impose, and 9.6 terminates every game. What is missing
without `ABC-021` is the player's ability to *exercise* a right — half a rule, not a broken one.

## The coupling: one ticket, and the split is refused

QE named it and it is treated as non-negotiable. The automatic fifty-move rule is currently the only
thing that terminates a locked position — QE drove
`8/8/8/p1p1p1p1/P1P1P1P1/8/8/K6k w - - 0 1` for **117 plies** to prove it. Remove the automation
without 9.6 and that game never ends.

**So both halves are in ONE ticket.** That is the mechanism, not a note in the margin: they land
together because they are one merge. Three things enforce it:

- `ABC-020` acceptance **A5** drives QE's exact locked-wall FEN and requires an automatic terminal
  verdict **within 150 plies**, naming 75-move as the verdict. It is unsatisfiable by reasoning and
  it is the criterion that fails if anyone separates the halves.
- The ticket carries an explicit anti-split clause: *"If a design hop or an engineer proposes
  splitting this ticket, the split is refused."*
- Size is `M`, so the no-`XL` rule requires no split and none is available.

**A second, quieter termination guarantee is recorded** because it decides two other rankings:
9.6.2 is **clock-based**. A repetition loop can contain neither a capture nor a pawn move — that is
what makes it a repetition — so the halfmove clock climbs monotonically through one. Termination at
150 plies therefore survives *any* under-detection in the Zobrist key (`ABC-011`) and *any* omitted
counts map (`ADR-0002` clause 4's optional parameter). `ABC-020` acceptance **A8** pins the second
of those as a test rather than leaving it as an argument.

## The two reversals, said out loud rather than edited around

This ruling reverses decisions made twice: `ABC-007` `## Out of scope` (fivefold and 75-move "not
needed") and `ABC-019` `## Out of scope` ("*Settled by merged code plus `EPIC-006`'s acceptance, not
open*"). **Neither was wrong when it was made.** QE verified `ABC-007`'s premise rather than taking
it on trust (R-6: a knight cycle from the start position lands its first verdict at occurrence 3
with the clock at 20, so 5 and 150 are genuinely unreachable *while the draws stay automatic*). Both
were made under a priority that no longer applies.

**Both `## Out of scope` blocks were amended in place, the same day, in the same commit as the
tickets.** The stale sentences are **struck rather than deleted**, each followed by a dated
amendment naming what superseded it and why — so a reader who finds either sentence quoted elsewhere
can trace it forward, and no two documents in this repo disagree with no explanation. `ABC-019`
additionally has one acceptance criterion amended, one added, one scope bullet amended and one
dependency added; its size, priority, eight states, fifteen transitions, stub seams, intra-ply
ordering, EOF ruling and every NFR bar are **unchanged**.

## What `ABC-020` is NOT

**It is not a defect ticket, and the trace row says so.** `src/gameend.ts` does exactly what
`ABC-007` specified; that work passed a review gate and a security/NFR gate and was merged on
explicit human instruction. Nothing escaped a gate. What changed is the specification. Filing this
as a defect would misattribute a scope change to an engineer and would make the gate-pass-rate metric
lie.

## The one thing `ABC-020` cannot answer for itself

**How the claimable set reaches the caller.** `ADR-0002` clause 4 says *"the signature does not
change"* — in context that is about refusing to make `positionCounts` required, and this ticket adds
no parameter and changes no supplier. But a claimable set has to reach the caller, and the natural
mechanisms (grow `GameStatus`; a second exported query; a discriminated result) all touch the
returned value. **Flagged to Architect/Design as a design-hop item, not decided in dev, and if it
turns out to be a clause amendment rather than an extension it goes to a named human — no agent may
amend a ratified clause.** It does not block: every acceptance criterion is written on observable
behaviour and resolves identically under all three options, the same posture `ABC-010` and `ABC-011`
already carry.

## Two items QE left open that this ruling partly closes

- **Terminal-condition count (`...claim-aab64fc7` §4).** `EPIC-006`'s "six" becomes **eight**
  distinct reasons: repetition splits into threefold-claimed and fivefold-automatic, fifty-move into
  fifty-move-claimed and 75-move-automatic. `EPIC-006` is amended to record that; its acceptance
  criterion is left reading "six" **deliberately**, because what that criterion tests (the reason is
  named, exit 0) is unchanged, and swapping a stale count for a guessed one before that epic is
  decomposed buys nothing. A draw-offer command would make it nine and this product has none.
- **G-3 (dead position, `...observation-36373ca4`).** Full FIDE 5.2.2 detection is **declined** —
  unbounded, no engine implements it, error direction safe. QE's recommendation was *documentation,
  not code*, and it is folded into `ABC-020`'s scope as a doc-comment correction (there is no FIDE
  article called "insufficient material"; it is a subset of 5.2.2) because that ticket rewrites the
  same header anyway. The delay bound moves from ~100 plies to 150 and stays bounded.
- **G-4** is carried into `ABC-020`'s `## Out of scope` **as a warning**, not as work: do not "fix"
  the stalemate-in-a-dead-position label by re-ordering the checks. R-4 records that ordering as
  load-bearing, and acceptance **A6** pins it at clock 100 *and* at clock 150.
