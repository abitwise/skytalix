## Size `M` held, and why that is not a rubber stamp

13 criteria became 22 and the row count went to 52, so the question is real. `M` holds because
**every added criterion is a row in a table `## Scope` already required — not new behaviour.** The
implementation is the same implementation: same grammar, same four disambiguation levels, same two
suffixes. What changed is that the engineer now receives 18 FENs and 52 expected strings *in the
ticket* instead of having to invent positions from prose and grade themselves. That moves work **out
of** the build. `XL`-split does not apply and no `SPLIT_REQUIRED` is raised.

## The case for REFUSING, which I took seriously and rejected

Refusing was a legitimate outcome and I nearly took it. The argument: **the acceptance table is now
the ticket's only oracle, and the instrument that validated it was written by the role that wrote
it.** That is exactly the dependency `ABC-023`'s QE hop broke, where an independent harness found
fourteen more surviving builds. By this board's base rate — five, seven, seven, six and now
eighteen defects against pre-checks naming three, four, two, three and four — the probability that
this table is complete is low.

I rejected it for one reason: **that argument does not identify an unmet Definition-of-Ready row.**
The bar is "acceptance criteria clear, testable and measurable", and all 22 are. Completeness is not
on the checklist, is not knowable before a mutation hop, and holding the ticket for it would apply a
standard no ticket on this board has ever met or could meet — which would make `Ready` a column
things enter and never leave. The independent check is QE's mutation hop, and Workflow 04 puts it
after dev, not before it.

**What would have made me refuse, stated so it can be checked against my behaviour:** a criterion I
could not make executable (there was none); a bar no correct build could meet that I could not
re-derive (I re-derived it); an unresolved dependency or a decision needing a human (there is none);
or a defect implying `## Scope` itself was wrong, which is a re-cut, not a repair (nothing found
pushed on Scope — every correction brought acceptance up to a Scope bullet already there).

**Confidence on this note is `medium`, not `high`,** and the reason is the paragraph above rather
than any specific doubt: the promotion rests on a table whose completeness I explicitly do not
certify. The DoR verdict is high-confidence; the implied "this ticket is now safe to build against"
is not, and those two should not be blurred.
