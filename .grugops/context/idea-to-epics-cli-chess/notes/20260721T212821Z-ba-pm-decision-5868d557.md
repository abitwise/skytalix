**User, pain, value.**

- *User*: someone at a terminal who wants a real game of chess right now — typically a developer, chess-curious, already in a shell.
- *Pain*: the alternatives all break flow. Browser chess means a context switch, an account, and ads. A serious engine means installing Stockfish plus a GUI. Existing terminal chess is usually either too weak to stay interesting past a few games, or has controls that assume fluent algebraic notation.
- *Value*: one command, five seconds, and you are playing an opponent that opens with real theory and punishes a hung piece — without needing to know chess notation to enter a move.

**MVP scope.** A complete, correct, playable human-vs-engine game: rules core (EPIC-001), terminal board and loop (EPIC-002), forgiving dual-format input (EPIC-003), club-strength engine (EPIC-004), bundled opening book (EPIC-005), session commands and npx packaging (EPIC-006).

**Non-goals — declined deliberately, not deferred.** Saying these out loud is what keeps the MVP defensible:

- No network or multiplayer play of any kind.
- No chess variants (Chess960, atomic, etc.).
- No UCI protocol support and no driving an external engine — this is a self-contained engine.
- No neural-network / NNUE evaluation; no endgame tablebases.
- No strength above ~1700.
- No GUI or web front end.
- No accounts, cloud sync, ratings, or a stored game database.
- No PGN analysis mode or engine-analysis view.

**The negotiable slice.** Save/resume and PGN export sit inside EPIC-006 and are explicitly *not* MVP. They earn their place only if play-testing shows people abandon games mid-way. They are the first thing to cut if that epic runs long.

**Priority ordering.** EPIC-001 through EPIC-004 are P0 — each is load-bearing for a playable, challenging game. EPIC-005 is P1: the game is fully playable without a book, just more repetitive and slower to open. EPIC-006 is P2: polish and distribution.
