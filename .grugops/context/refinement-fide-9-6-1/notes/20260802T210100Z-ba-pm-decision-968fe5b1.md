# RULING: FIDE 9.6.1 does NOT say "consecutive". The code conforms. No gap, no ticket, open question 1 CLOSED.

## The ruling in one line

The current Laws say **"at least five times"** with no consecutiveness requirement of any kind, so
`src/gameend.ts`'s plain five-occurrence automatic draw is **conformant**. No ticket is cut and
nothing in `src/` changes.

## The authoritative text, quoted exactly

Retrieved by this role from FIDE's own handbook, `https://handbook.fide.com/chapter/E012023`,
HTTP 200, 2026-08-02. Page `<title>`: *FIDE Handbook FIDE Laws of Chess taking effect from
1 January 2023*. Quoted verbatim, including its punctuation:

> 9.6 If one or both of the following occur(s) then the game is drawn:
>
> 9.6.1 the same position has appeared, as in 9.2.2 at least five times.
>
> 9.6.2 any series of at least 75 moves have been made by each player without the movement of any
> pawn and without any capture. If the last move resulted in checkmate, that shall take precedence.

The word **"consecutive" appears zero times in the entire 2023 Laws of Chess** — not in Article 9,
not anywhere in the chapter. That was measured on the retrieved bytes, not recalled.

## Why QE remembered it, and why QE was right to refuse to assert it

QE's remembered sentence was **real, verbatim, and two editions stale**. The same handbook still
publishes the superseded editions, and the wording changed exactly once:

| Edition (FIDE's own `<title>`) | Article 9.6.1 as published |
|---|---|
| `E012014` — *For competitions starting from 1 July 2014 till 30 June 2017* | "the same position has appeared, as in 9.2b, **for at least five consecutive alternate moves by each player**." |
| `E012017` — *1 July 2017 till 31 December 2017* | "the same position has appeared, as in 9.2.2 **at least five times**." |
| `E012018` — *1 January 2018 till 31 December 2022* | "the same position has appeared, as in 9.2.2 **at least five times**." |
| `E012023` — *taking effect from 1 January 2023* (CURRENT) | "the same position has appeared, as in 9.2.2 **at least five times**." |

The "consecutive alternate moves" wording was removed effective **1 July 2017** and has been absent
from three successive editions since. `E012023` is the newest Laws chapter the handbook index
publishes, so it is the operative text as of 2026-08-02.

**QE's `UNKNOWN - verify` was the correct call and this ruling vindicates it rather than overturning
it.** A remembered sentence produced the right words attached to the wrong edition — which is
precisely the failure mode that ruling-from-memory cannot detect, and precisely why the plain
five-count "most implementations do" is not evidence either. Both halves of QE's refusal were sound.

## Applying the text to the code

`src/gameend.ts:154-159` reads the current position's Zobrist key out of `positionCounts` and draws
automatically when that count reaches five:

```
const occurrences = positionCounts === undefined ? 0 : (positionCounts.get(zobristKey(position)) ?? 0);
if (occurrences >= FIVEFOLD_OCCURRENCES) { return { result: "draw", reason: "fivefold-repetition" }; }
```

Against "the same position has appeared … at least five times" this is a **term-for-term match**:

- "at least five" → `>= 5`, not `=== 5`. The inequality is the correct direction and the strict
  equality would have been the defect.
- "the same position has appeared … times" → a **count of appearances**, which is exactly what a
  counts map is. The Laws impose no ordering predicate, so there is nothing an ordered structure
  could express that the map is missing.
- "as in 9.2.2" is a cross-reference to the **manner of appearance** — 9.2.2 reads "has just
  appeared, and the player claiming the draw has the move" — and it pulls in 9.2.3's definition of
  when two positions are the same. It is **not** a consecutiveness requirement, and on the current
  text nothing else in Article 9 supplies one.

**The premise of the whole open question therefore fails.** There is no reading of the current
9.6.1 on which five non-consecutive occurrences should be left to the 9.2 claim. The feared error
direction — an automatic draw taken from the player having the move, the G-1 class ABC-020 fixed —
**does not exist here**, because the Laws themselves make this draw automatic on a plain count.
