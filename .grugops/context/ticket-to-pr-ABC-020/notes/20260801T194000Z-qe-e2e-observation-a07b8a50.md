# Ruling on the Engineer's A5 epoch argument: it is CORRECT, not a rationalisation — and my own G-3 wording was the thing that was wrong

This was the item I was asked to check hardest, because a plausible story is exactly how a real gap
gets papered over. **It is not a story. Every number in it reproduces, and the one imprecision runs
against the Engineer, not for it.**

## The claim under review

The Engineer's `decision` §1 and `observation` note argue that A5's *"within 150 plies"* is a bound
**per irreversible-move epoch**, not from the starting FEN; that QE's published *"terminal after 117
plies"* is explained by a clock reset at ply 17 (`150 − 133 = 17`, `17 + 100 = 117`); and that A5's
test therefore drives the no-progress line the criterion's `Given` describes rather than an
arbitrary legal line.

## What I measured, on BOTH trees

Driving the wall `8/8/8/p1p1p1p1/P1P1P1P1/8/8/K6k w - - 0 1` under five policies, on the branch and
on the pre-`ABC-020` tree (`b1b96cd~1`, i.e. merged semantics):

```
  PRE-ABC-020 (automatic fifty-move)          THIS BRANCH (claimable fifty-move, 9.6 automatic)
  first-legal, bare  117 plies clock=100      first-legal, bare  167 plies clock=150
                     {"draw","fifty-move"}                       {"draw","seventy-five-move"}
  last-legal,  bare  100 plies clock=100      last-legal,  bare  150 plies clock=150
  quiet-only,  bare  100 plies clock=100      quiet-only,  bare  150 plies clock=150
  first-legal, +counts  29 plies threefold    first-legal, +counts  37 plies fivefold-repetition
  quiet-only,  +counts  19 plies threefold    quiet-only,  +counts  27 plies fivefold-repetition

  first-legal at ply 150 : clock = 133, clock resets at plies [8, 14, 17]
```

**The arithmetic is exact, three ways.** `150 − 133 = 17`. `17 + 100 = 117` — and 117 is precisely
what the pre-`ABC-020` tree returns under the first-legal policy, reproducing my own published G-3
figure to the ply. `17 + 150 = 167` — and 167 is precisely what this branch returns under the same
policy. The Engineer inferred my drive policy from one identity and was right.

**The one imprecision is the Engineer's, and it understates rather than overstates.** The note says
*"the clock reset at ply 17 — a king took a pawn"*. There are **three** resets, not one:

```
  reset at ply  8: capture=true   pawnMove=false
  reset at ply 14: capture=false  pawnMove=TRUE
  reset at ply 17: capture=true   pawnMove=false
```

Only the **last** matters for the arithmetic, so the conclusion is untouched. The ply-14 row is the
interesting one: it is a **pawn move on a wall described as frozen**. Once the ply-8 capture removes
a white pawn, the black pawn behind it is unblocked. The wall does not merely permit one capture —
it **unfreezes**.

## The wording that is actually wrong is mine

`ABC-020`'s A5 `Given` says the wall has *"three legal king moves, forever, no capture and no pawn
move available to either side"*. That sentence is carried verbatim from my own G-3 note of
2026-07-31. **The first clause is true only at the starting position and the last is false beyond
ply 7.** Both kings start on rank 1 below the wall and share that half-board; a king that walks to
rank 3 can step onto rank 4 and take a loose white pawn, and after that the pawns move.

I published it, the Engineer refuted it with a drive, and the refutation is right. Recording that
plainly: **the defect is in the ticket's `Given`, inherited from my note, not in the code and not in
the implementation's reading of it.**

## Ruling, stated in the form I was asked for

**The epoch argument is right and A5 is met in substance.** FIDE 9.6.2 bounds *"any consecutive
series of 75 moves ... without the movement of any pawn and without any capture"* — a bound per
epoch, which is exactly what the code implements and exactly what the Engineer says it is. Read
literally against an arbitrary legal line, A5's *"within 150 plies"* is **false** (167), and A5's
*"and that verdict is the 75-move draw"* is **false for a history-carrying caller** (fivefold, at
ply 27–37, which is correct FIDE and arrives sooner). The Engineer disclosed both narrowings in
writing instead of quietly satisfying the criterion — which is the behaviour this review wants — and
neither narrowing weakens the criterion's substance.

**And the substance is the coupling, which holds under everything I could throw at it.** A5 exists
to prove that removing the automatic fifty-move rule does not produce a game that never ends. Ten
drives — five policies × two trees — every one terminates. Termination is not an artifact of the
Engineer's move filter: it is guaranteed because captures (≤ 30) and pawn moves (≤ 96) are both
finite, so the clock must eventually reach 150 whatever anyone plays, and a caller holding a history
hits 9.6.1 sooner still. **The two halves of this ticket had to land together and they did.**

## What I did about it, since a note is weaker than a test

Two of my six added tests pin this rather than leaving it in prose:

- **the last-legal-move drive** — a second, independent no-progress line, terminal at exactly ply
  150 with `seventy-five-move`; proves A5's result is not an artifact of its first-quiet-move filter;
- **the first-legal-move drive** — a line that *does* capture, asserting that the game still ends
  automatically, that `plies > 150` (so the bound is provably **not** from the starting FEN), and
  that `plies − lastIrreversibleMove === 150` (so the bound provably **is** per epoch).

The second test is the one that matters: the epoch reading is now a failing test if anyone
"corrects" it, and the non-terminating game is now unreachable by assertion and not only by
argument.

## Recommendation, which is not mine to take

`ABC-020`'s A5 and A8 `Given` clauses should be corrected by BA/PM: the wall is not permanently
locked, and the bound is 150 plies per irreversible-move epoch. **The criteria themselves need no
change** — they are met — and the code needs none. This is a documentation defect on the ticket,
originating in my own note, and it is one sentence.
